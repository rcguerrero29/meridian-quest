# How it's made — the process is the shape

*Opened 2026-09-16, after four attempts at a slice of gimbap. The fourth was accurate about the
object and still wrong, and the owner said why in one sentence:*

> *"think about how it is made too right? so the roll should make sense in terms of how it is rolled
> up and the sliced into round slices — thats why rarely are they displayed like you did or if it
> close it is in a pile and they have more congruence"*

**He was right, and the error was bigger than every colour and proportion I had been fixing.** I had
drawn six slices of gimbap as six independent objects, each with its own randomly arranged filling.
But all six came off **one roll**. The fillings were laid in a line on a flat sheet of nori, rolled
once, and then cut — so the cross-section is *the same all the way down the cylinder*. Six slices of
one roll are **siblings**, not strangers. Mine were six different rolls with one slice each, and no
amount of correcting the bundle was ever going to fix that.

---

## The rule, and it is one sentence

> **Variation enters at the step it actually entered, and nowhere earlier.**

Anything decided before the copies were separated is **shared by all of them**. Anything decided
after is what differs. Get this backwards and the result reads as *generated* — and nobody will be
able to tell you why, because every individual object is fine.

In the gimbap:

| step | when it happened | so across six slices it is |
|---|---|---|
| which fillings, and in what order | **once**, on the flat sheet | **identical** |
| how tightly it was rolled | **once** | **identical** |
| where the knife fell | per slice | **different** — this is the only honest source of size variation |
| how it landed on the plate | per slice | **different** — rotation, lean, overlap |

So: one filling arrangement, six times, at six rotations, at six thicknesses. That is the picture.
Six arrangements is a picture of something that cannot exist.

## The five questions, before you draw anything

**1 · How was it made? Say the steps out loud.**
Not "it is a bowl" — *thrown on a wheel, trimmed, glazed, fired*. The steps are the answer sheet for
everything below.

**2 · What does each step leave behind?**
Every process leaves a signature, and the signature is most of what makes a drawing convincing:

| process | what it leaves |
|---|---|
| **rolled** | a spiral, a seam where the sheet ends, a cross-section that repeats along the whole length |
| **cut / sliced** | a flat face at an angle you chose, tool marks across it, and **a face that is the same on every slice from the same piece** |
| **thrown on a wheel** | concentric rings inside, a foot ring, a slight wobble off true |
| **cast or moulded** | a mould seam, a sprue mark, identical siblings and *no* honest variation at all |
| **extruded** | a constant cross-section, a cut at each end |
| **woven** | a warp you see and a weft you half see, and a selvedge at the edges |
| **stacked or piled** | the ones underneath are squashed; the top one is not |
| **poured** | a meniscus, a level surface finding gravity, a rim where it stopped |
| **grown** | asymmetry that follows a direction — light, gravity, a stem — never random asymmetry |
| **worn / used** | the damage is where the hands go, and nowhere else |

**3 · What do the copies share?**
From one batch, one roll, one mould, one sheet: what is common? Draw the common thing ONCE and
reuse it. This is where most "generated" art fails.

**4 · How did it come to rest?**
Objects do not float in a row at equal spacing. They **lean, shingle, stack, sag, roll, touch, and
settle against each other**. A cut cylinder standing on its face topples into its neighbour. If your
arrangement is evenly spaced and upright, ask what is holding it there — and if the answer is
"nothing", that is a drawing of a diagram.

**5 · What did the tool leave?**
Knife marks, brush direction, finger dents in clay, the drip on the glaze, the flat spot where it sat
while it cooled. One of these, placed where it belongs, is worth more than an hour of colour work.

## The tell, in reverse

When a picture of several things reads as generated and nobody can say why, check in this order:

1. **Are the copies siblings or strangers?** Strangers that should be siblings is the commonest fault
   and the hardest to see, because every individual object is correct.
2. **Is anything randomised that was decided before the copies separated?**
3. **Are they arranged, or are they just placed?** Evenly spaced, non-touching, all upright is the
   signature of a loop that ran `n` times.
4. **Does any surface show the tool?**

## And the smaller lesson that came with it

Three attempts before this one were spent correcting the object — the nori's thickness, the size of
the bundle, the exact yellow of the danmuji — and every correction was right and none of them was the
problem. **Reasoning from the NAME of a thing produces a diagram of the name.** Reasoning from how it
is made produces the thing.

If you cannot reach a reference, say so and ask for one. That should happen on the second failed
attempt, not the fourth.
