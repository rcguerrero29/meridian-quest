## 1 · FINDINGS

### A. How eating habits actually form

**1. A habit is context-dependent repetition, it takes about ten weeks not three, and missing a day does not matter.** Lally et al. 2010: 96 volunteers repeated one eating/drinking/activity behaviour in the same context daily for 12 weeks; median 66 days to automaticity, range 18–254, and only ~48% reached it (simpler behaviours fastest); "missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process." `[WEB]` https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674 · https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit · https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit
**For this game:** the unit is *one small action tied to one existing moment* ("after breakfast", "Sunday afternoon"), and the horizon is a season, which the engine already has (`SEASONS`). Streaks are contradicted by the primary study that everyone cites for them — see #14.

**2. The clinically-used advice is three sentences: pick a small behaviour, pick a cue you already meet daily, repeat.** Gardner, Lally & Wardle 2012 (BJGP) turn #1 into GP advice with a patient leaflet and weekly self-rating of automaticity. `[WEB]` https://bjgp.org/content/62/605/664
**For this game:** the "plan" screen should ask *when and where*, not *how much*. A character can carry the leaflet's voice.

**3. Knowledge and good intentions produce short-term change; durable change needs repetition (≥1 month), stable cues, and rewards — especially uncertain ones — plus friction against the old habit.** Wood & Neal 2016 review: habit-forming = repetition + context cues + intermittent rewards; habit-breaking = cue disruption, environmental re-engineering (add friction to the unwanted, remove it from the wanted), vigilant monitoring. `[WEB]` https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/237946151600200109 · PDF https://dornsife.usc.edu/wendy-wood/wp-content/uploads/sites/183/2023/10/Wood.Neal_.2016.pdf
**For this game:** rewards should be warm and *unpredictable* (a neighbour turns up with a story), not a ladder (GENRE-RULES R6). "Pantry organisation" has real evidence only as *friction design* — where things live — not as decoration.

**4. Context change opens a window: people re-decide habits when they move house, and interventions land better then; temporal landmarks (Monday, new month, birthdays) do a smaller version.** Verplanken & Roy 2016 field experiment; Milkman/Dai "fresh start effect". `[WEB]` https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272494415300487 · https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/spc3.12845 · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK593511/
**For this game:** the town already has occasions/seasons; a *new season* or *a new shelf* is the correct moment to offer one new habit, and the only moment worth a prompt.

**5. If-then plans ("if it is Sunday, then I soak beans") work — better for adding a healthy food than for cutting an unhealthy one.** Adriaanse et al. 2011, meta-analysis of 23 studies: implementation intentions are "somewhat more effective in promoting healthy eating than in diminishing unhealthy eating", with a caveat that some promotion effects were inflated by weak controls. `[WEB]` https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21056605/ · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0195666318314363
**For this game:** every goal is framed as *adding* (a vegetable, a home-cooked dinner), never *removing*. This is the same rule as GENRE-RULES R2: a mistake is only a punishment if it subtracts.

**6. Habit-based advice is cheap and real, and its effect is modest.** 10 Top Tips RCT (Beeken et al. 2017): 537 adults with obesity in 14 English practices; the habit leaflet lost 0.87 kg more than usual care at 3 months; by 24 months both arms were similar; cost ≈ £23 per person. `[WEB]` https://www.nature.com/articles/ijo2016206 · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5300101/
**For this game:** the strongest habit intervention in a health service produced under a kilo. **A game must never promise weight.**

### B. Cooking at home, planning, food literacy, company

**7. People who cook dinner at home most nights eat measurably better — cross-sectionally.** Wolfson & Bleich 2015 (NHANES 2007–10, n≈9,569): those cooking dinner 6–7×/week averaged 2,164 kcal, 81 g fat, 119 g sugar vs 2,301 kcal, 84 g, 135 g for ≤1×/week, and ate fewer calories when they did eat out; a later paper finds higher HEI-2015 with more frequent cooking. Both cross-sectional. `[WEB]` https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728746/ · https://hub.jhu.edu/gazette/2015/january-february/currents-home-cooking-is-healthier/ · https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/public-health-nutrition/article/more-frequent-cooking-at-home-is-associated-with-higher-healthy-eating-index2015-score/A6515CE705AA635DE9EB02FC4384F766
**For this game:** the core verb the owner wants (cook at home) is the one with evidence behind it. Say "associated with", never "causes".

**8. Meal planning is associated with more food variety, better guideline adherence and lower odds of overweight.** Ducrot et al. 2017, NutriNet-Santé, n=40,554; 57% plan at least occasionally; lower odds of overweight and obesity in women, obesity in men; cross-sectional. `[WEB]` https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5288891/ · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12966-017-0461-7
**For this game:** "organize future meals" is the feature with evidence. **Planning is not counting** — the plan is a list of dishes, not a budget.

**9. A meal plan + a shopping list + storage advice cuts household food waste, fast.** Coaching on plan/list/storage cut vegetable waste 38–49%; merely reading an article plus a planning tool cut waste 24% in a week; "make a shopping list" and "freeze excess food" rank as high-impact and easy. `[WEB]` https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666784323000438 · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301479723014561 · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK564041/
**For this game:** the pantry's honest job is *a list and a freezer*. Waste is a goal that is about the kitchen and not the body — it is the calm goal, and it is bilingual for free.

**10. Cooking confidence is the outcome that predicts diet years later; food-literacy programmes move confidence and vegetables, on weak designs.** Utter et al. 2018 (longitudinal, 10 years): adequate cooking skills in emerging adulthood predicted ≥3 vegetable servings/day and less fast food later. 2024 scoping review: food literacy = plan/manage · select · prepare/cook · eat; interventions improve behaviour and diet quality but most are single-group. `[WEB]` https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6086120/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11350956/ · https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0355940 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6655530/
**For this game:** design for *"I can do this"* — a character who has watched you make it twice — rather than a score. The four literacy domains are a ready map of four rooms.

**11. Eating with others is associated with better intake and lower depression risk; every one of the four guideline systems below names it.** 2026 meta-analysis (older adults): solitary eating → lower energy, protein, and higher psychological risk; social facilitation lengthens meals. `[WEB]` https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13402156/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8036467/
**For this game:** a town with named neighbours is *already* the evidence-shaped feature. "Who is this for?" (the 2026-09-11 sweep's question (b)) is also the nutrition answer.

### C. Tracking, calorie counting, restriction

**12. Self-monitoring is associated with weight loss in trials, and in the wild almost nobody keeps doing it.** Burke 2011 review: consistent association, weak methods. Helander 2014: of 189,770 downloads of a free photo food-diary app, 2.58% were "active" (≥10 photos over ≥1 week). `[WEB]` https://www.jandonline.org/article/s0002-8223(10)01644-5/abstract · https://www.jmir.org/2014/4/e109/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4004142/
**For this game:** a loop built on logging inherits ~97% churn. Do not build a logger.

**13. Calorie counting is contraindicated for a meaningful slice of adults, and rigid control predicts disordered eating; intuitive eating predicts the opposite.** Levinson 2017: of 105 people with an eating disorder, ~75% used MyFitnessPal and 73% of those felt it contributed. Rigid (vs flexible) dietary control relates to disordered eating and body concern; Linardon 2021 meta-analysis (97 studies, 89% cross-sectional): intuitive eating ↔ less binge/restraint, more body appreciation and self-compassion. `[WEB]` https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700836/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28131005/ · https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/eat.23509 · https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/eat.24192
**For this game:** **no calorie totals, no weight entry, no "over budget" red.** If numbers appear at all they are optional, about *kinds* (food groups, plants), never about kcal.

**14. Streaks are loss aversion with a costume, and the vendors who sell them ship "streak freezes" because they know it.** The science is #1 (a missed day does not matter); the industry sources are vendors and say so themselves. `[WEB]` vendor: https://trophy.so/blog/streaks-feature-gamification-examples · https://www.professorgame.com/podcast/423/
**For this game:** no streaks. This is GENRE-RULES R11 (no punishment for stepping away) restated as nutrition.

**15. Gamification's evidence is real, mixed, and mostly about steps, not food.** Koivisto & Hamari 2019 (819 studies): leans positive, "the amount of mixed results is remarkable"; points/badges/leaderboards dominate. eClinicalMedicine 2024 RCT meta-analysis: gamified vs non-gamified apps → more steps, lower weight/BMI/waist, but inconsistent. The nutrition-specific meta-analysis is in *children* (fruit/veg and knowledge up). SDT meta-analysis (education): gamification raises autonomy and relatedness, minimal effect on competence. `[WEB]` https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0268401217305169 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11701442/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8308535/ · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7
**For this game:** what gamification demonstrably supplies is *relatedness and autonomy* — which characters and choice give — not points. The evidence does not license a leaderboard and does not need one.

### D. Environment and pantry

**16. The one environment lever with Cochrane-grade evidence is size: bigger portions, packages and plates → people eat more, regardless of BMI or restraint.** Hollands 2015: 61 studies, 6,711 participants. `[WEB]` https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD011045.pub2/full · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26368271/
**For this game:** "decant into smaller jars / serve from smaller bowls" is sayable and sourced. It is also a pixel-art-shaped fact.

**17. Almost every popular "kitchen design" tip descends from Brian Wansink, whose work was retracted (≈18 papers) after a Cornell misconduct finding.** "Cluttered kitchens make you overeat", "kitchenscapes", visible-fruit-bowl claims are compromised. `[WEB]` https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/stephaniemlee/brian-wansink-jama-six-retractions-cornell · https://www.theglobeandmail.com/life/health-and-fitness/article-despite-retracted-papers-concepts-about-overeating-still-carry-some/ · https://www.npr.org/sections/thesalt/2016/02/15/466567647/a-cluttered-kitchen-can-nudge-us-to-overeat-study-finds (the retracted-era claim, kept as the example)
**For this game:** never cite Wansink; the pantry's evidence is #3 (friction) and #16 (size). "Keep fruit where you can see it" stays `[TRAINING]` — plausible, not proven.

### E. What "healthy" means, by guideline

**18. WHO's four numbers for adults: ≥400 g fruit and vegetables a day (five portions); free sugars <10% of energy, ideally <5%; salt <5 g; fat <30% with saturated <10% and trans <1%.** `[WEB]` https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/healthy-diet/healthy-diet-fact-sheet-394.pdf · https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet
**For this game:** these are population targets a character may quote with the link; they are not personal prescriptions.

**19. MyPlate / Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2020–25: half the plate fruit and vegetables; six core elements; added sugars <10%, saturated fat <10%, sodium <2,300 mg.** The MyPlate icon is public domain, no permission needed, may not imply USDA endorsement. A 2025–2030 DGA now exists (not read). `[WEB]` https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/sites/default/files/2021-03/Dietary_Guidelines_for_Americans-2020-2025.pdf · https://ask.usda.gov/s/article/Am-I-permitted-to-use-content-or-materials-from-ChooseMyPlate-gov · https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002093.htm
**For this game:** the plate is the one guideline graphic that is legally trivial to redraw in pixels.

**20. The Mediterranean pyramid (Bach-Faig 2011, Mediterranean Diet Foundation) is the only major guideline whose *base* is conviviality, cooking, seasonality, traditional/local products, activity and rest — i.e., the things this game does.** Plant-based core; PREDIMED and cohorts behind it; UNESCO heritage 2010; Italy's CREA 2018 guidelines adopt the Mediterranean model and add "Italian standard portions". `[WEB]` https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23258395/ · https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(25)00259-X/fulltext · https://www.crea.gov.it/en/web/alimenti-e-nutrizione/-/linee-guida-per-una-sana-alimentazione-2018 · https://www.epicentro.iss.it/alimentazione/linee-guida-sana-alimentazione-crea-2018
**For this game:** the Italian half is easiest to make both healthy and calm because its own guideline says *sit down together and cook*.

**21. Japan frames health in dishes, not nutrients: the Food Guide Spinning Top (2005/2010) counts grain, vegetable, fish/meat, milk, fruit *dishes*, with a running figure on top; the 2016 Dietary Guidelines for Japanese carry ten messages including enjoy meals, keep a rhythm, honour washoku, and reduce food loss; the 2005 Basic Act on Shokuiku makes food education include culture, season and eating together.** Cohort evidence: JPHC, n=92,969, 18.9 years — top vs bottom Japanese Diet Index HR 0.86 all-cause, 0.89 CVD. Caveat in the same literature: sodium (miso, soy, pickles) ↔ stroke, HR 1.55 top vs bottom quintile. `[WEB]` https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/japan/en/ · https://www.maff.go.jp/j/syokuiku/attach/pdf/shishinn-10.pdf · https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/3419/en · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7987617/ · https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.str.0000130425.50441.b0
**For this game:** *ichijū-sansai* (one soup, three sides) is a game-shaped unit — a plate you compose, no counting — and the salt caveat is where a character teaches honestly instead of flattering the cuisine.

**22. Korea: the Food Balance Wheels (a bicycle with a water cup) with six groups since 2020, and nine general dietary guidelines; the national caveat is sodium (~4.6 g/day, from kimchi, soy sauce, pastes), with one study finding kimchi itself not associated with hypertension, plausibly via potassium.** Kimjang is UNESCO heritage (2013). `[WEB]` https://www.researchgate.net/figure/The-Korean-Food-Balance-Wheels-Source-Dietary-Reference-Intakes-for-Koreans-First_fig1_235670321 · https://onav.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/General-Dietary-Guidelines-for-Koreans.pdf · https://e-nrp.org/DOIx.php?id=10.4162%2Fnrp.2021.15.3.382 · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352618114000055
**For this game:** *banchan* is the same composable-plate mechanic as #21; "a cup of water on the wheel" is a free, sourced image.

**23. Mexico's 2023 Guías Alimentarias redrew the Plato del Bien Comer as 50% vegetables and fruit, 22% cereals, 15% legumes, 8% animal foods, 5% oils, with ten recommendations: plain water not sugary drinks, less red/processed meat and ultra-processed food, regional traditional recipes, breastfeeding, sustainability.** Published by the Secretaría de Salud with INSP and others; traditional Mexican cuisine is UNESCO heritage (2010). `[WEB]` https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/Mexico/es · https://www.ciad.mx/nuevas-guias-alimentarias-para-la-poblacion-mexicana-y-el-plato-del-bien-comer/ · https://alianzasalud.org.mx/2023/04/presenta-ssa-nuevas-guias-alimentarias-2023/ · https://movendi.ngo/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Gui_as_Alimentarias_2023_para_la_poblacio_n_mexicana.pdf
**For this game:** the Mexican guideline *explicitly* asks for traditional recipes and regional foods — a legume-forward plate is both the guideline and the cuisine (frijoles, nixtamal). The bilingual copy for this can quote the official Spanish.

**24. "Less ultra-processed" is in every guideline above and has an umbrella review behind it, but it is observational and NOVA is a blunt category.** Lane et al. BMJ 2024: 45 pooled analyses, ~9.9 M people, 32 outcomes; convincing for CVD mortality, type 2 diabetes, anxiety. `[WEB]` https://bmjgroup.com/consistent-evidence-links-ultra-processed-food-to-over-30-damaging-health-outcomes/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38688162/ · https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-umbrella-review-looking-at-ultra-processed-food-exposure-and-adverse-health-outcomes/
**For this game:** say it the way the guidelines do ("prefer foods you cooked"), never as a disease claim.

**25. If the game ever shows a "how healthy is this" signal, a letter grade is the most understood format, and the algorithm is open.** Egnell 2018, 12 countries, ~1,000 people each: Nutri-Score best at objective understanding among five front-of-pack labels; updated 2023; Open Food Facts computes it openly on 3 M+ products. `[WEB]` https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6213801/ · https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-024-00920-3 · https://blog.openfoodfacts.org/en/news/open-food-facts-computes-the-new-nutriscore-on-3-million-products
**For this game:** an ordinal (A–E, or a colour) over a number — if at all.

**26. "Count kinds, not calories" has a sourceable basis but only as association.** American Gut (2018): ≥30 plant types/week ↔ higher microbiome diversity; the popular sources are vendors (ZOE, tinyhealth). `[WEB]` vendor: https://zoe.com/learn/30-plants-per-week · https://wellnesspulse.com/nutrition/30-plants-a-week-gut-health/
**For this game:** variety is the one count that is calm, non-body, bilingual, and pixel-drawable (thirty sprites). Tag it "associated with".

**27. Seasonality is a cultural value in the Mediterranean and Japanese frames, and a weak nutrient claim.** Modern cultivars vary more by breed/feed than season. `[WEB]` https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-nutrition-society/article/seasonality-and-dietary-requirements-will-eating-seasonal-food-contribute-to-health-and-environmental-sustainability/08545F71A12EF0FE233E8D1DEFEF227A
**For this game:** seasons belong to the fiction (`SEASONS`), not to a health claim.

### F. Where the numbers may come from, and what a static game may embed

**28. USDA FoodData Central is CC0 / public domain — the safest thing to embed.** Attribution requested, not required; API key via data.gov; bulk CSV downloads; data types Foundation, SR Legacy (final, 2018), FNDDS (recipe-calculated dishes — the right one for *dishes*), Branded; English only. `[WEB]` https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/ · https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/fooddata-central · https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/data-documentation
**For this game:** a JSON subset (a few hundred ingredients) shipped inside a pack, with "Source: USDA FoodData Central" on the card. No runtime fetch needed, which matters for a GitHub Pages PWA.

**29. Open Food Facts is ODbL + DbCL (+ CC BY-SA images): attribution *and share-alike*; combining it with other databases makes the combined database ODbL; the docs themselves say there are "no assurances that the data is accurate".** API 15 product / 10 search requests per minute per IP, custom User-Agent required, bulk CSV/JSONL preferred. Coverage: Italy 263,739 products, Japan 35,345, Mexico 15,932, South Korea 2,709. `[WEB, read]` https://github.com/openfoodfacts/openfoodfacts-server/blob/main/docs/api/index.md · https://github.com/openfoodfacts/openfoodfacts-server/blob/main/README.md (code is AGPL: https://github.com/openfoodfacts/openfoodfacts-server/blob/main/LICENSE) · extract: https://world.openfoodfacts.org/terms-of-use · https://world.openfoodfacts.org/countries
**For this game:** fine for an open repo (the embedded subset ships under ODbL with a notice), but the moment it is merged with FDC/MEXT into one table, the *merged* table is ODbL. Korea's coverage is too thin to rely on. Best use: packaged products only, and at build time.

**30. Japan's Standard Tables (2020, 8th rev., 2,478 foods; supplement 2023) sit on MEXT's website terms, which follow the Government Standard Terms of Use v2.0 and are CC BY 4.0-compatible; cite the source.** Excel files exist. `[WEB]` https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/1351168.htm · https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/syokuhinseibun/mext_01110.html · https://fooddb.mext.go.jp/
**For this game:** the best-licensed national table after FDC; embeddable with attribution. Whether the table pages carry the site-wide terms was not confirmed (see §5).

**31. Korea's National Standard Food Composition DB 10.0 (3,272 foods, 130 nutrients, ~250k records) is released under KOGL Type 1 (attribution; commercial and derivative use allowed) — but the printed 10th-edition book is KOGL Type 2 (non-commercial), and the 9th revision was CC BY-NC.** `[WEB]` https://www.rda.go.kr/board/board.do?mode=view&prgId=day_farmprmninfoEntry&dataNo=100000778935 · https://www.data.go.kr/data/15123901/fileData.do · https://koreanfood.rda.go.kr/kfi/fct/fctIntro/list?menuId=PS03562
**For this game:** embed from the DB (Type 1), never transcribe from the book.

**32. Italy's CREA tables (900 foods, 20 categories, 120 nutrients) are free to consult and CREA asks re-users — scientific, educational, commercial — to "respect intellectual property and cite the original source"; there is no explicit open licence.** `[WEB]` https://www.alimentinutrizione.it/sezioni/tabelle-nutrizionali/ · https://www.crea.gov.it/en/-/tabella-di-composizione-degli-alimenti
**For this game:** link out or ask before embedding. For Italian ingredients FDC covers most of them anyway.

**33. Mexico has no clearly-licensed table: INCMNSZ's *Tablas de composición de alimentos y productos alimenticios mexicanos* (FAO lists a 2015 version; an "extended 2019" database is served through CONABIO's SiAgro) carries no licence I could find, and the SMAE (Sistema Mexicano de Alimentos Equivalentes) is a commercial book (Fomento de Nutrición y Salud; 4th ed. 2014, 5th ed. later).** `[WEB]` https://www.incmnsz.mx/2017/Tablas/index.html · https://www.fao.org/food-composition/tables-and-databases/detail/(mexico--2015)-tabla-de-composici%C3%B3n-de-alimentos-y-productos-alimenticios-mexicanos/en · https://siagro.conabio.gob.mx/nutricion/download/Detalles_tecnicos.pdf · https://www.amazon.com/SISTEMA-MEXICANO-ALIMENTOS-EQUIVALENTES-ED/dp/6070079280
**For this game:** do not embed either. Use FDC for nixtamal, chiles, frijol etc. (it has them) and cite the Mexican *guideline* (#23) for the framing. Ask INCMNSZ/CONABIO if the owner wants the national table.

**34. Recipes can be pasted: a list of ingredients and a simple set of directions is not copyrightable; the prose, photos and the compilation are.** US Copyright Office Circular 33. `[WEB]` https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf · https://copyrightalliance.org/are-recipes-cookbooks-protected-by-copyright/
**For this game:** the owner's "copy and paste some recipes" is legal if it is ingredients + steps in the game's own words, no blog prose, no photos — and GENRE-RULES R12 still says name the person.

### G. What the game may say

**35. A healthy-lifestyle app that makes no disease claim is "general wellness" and outside FDA device enforcement; a health claim in advertising needs "competent and reliable scientific evidence" — for health claims, typically RCTs — and a disclaimer does not cure a deceptive claim; in the EU, health claims need prior authorisation and claims about the rate or amount of weight loss are prohibited outright.** FDA General Wellness policy (2019, revised Jan 2026); FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022); Regulation (EC) 1924/2006. `[WEB]` https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices-guidance-industry-and-food-and-drug · https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products · https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance · https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/nutrition-and-health-claims-made-on-foods.html
**For this game:** "helps you plan, cook and keep a pantry" is safe; "lowers blood pressure", "lose 5 kg" is not, in any language.

**36. ## 2 · LIMITATIONS — what the evidence does not support, what backfires, what must not be promised

1. **Nothing here supports a weight-loss promise.** The best habit RCT (#6) produced <1 kg and washed out by 24 months; cooking and planning evidence (#7, #8) is cross-sectional. EU law prohibits rate/amount-of-weight-loss claims outright (#35).
2. **Calorie counting, tracking and streaks are the three mechanics most likely to harm the people most likely to use them** (#12–14). Ninety-seven percent of people stop logging; a quarter to three-quarters of people with eating disorders say the counter helped cause it. A calm game that adds a kcal meter has un-calmed itself.
3. **Gamification's evidence base is steps, not food, and is "remarkably" mixed** (#15). Points, badges and leaderboards are the most-used and least-distinguished elements. Do not cite gamification research as proof that gamifying eating works in adults — the adult nutrition RCTs are thin.
4. **Most "kitchen environment" tips are Wansink** (#17). The safe environment claims are portion/package size (#16) and friction (#3). "Keep fruit visible" is folk wisdom until somebody finds a non-Wansink source.
5. **"Healthy" is not one thing.** Japan and Korea's protective patterns come with a sodium problem the cohorts document (#21, #22); the Mediterranean pyramid is a lifestyle, not a nutrient list (#20); Mexico's 2023 plate is 15% legumes — a bigger share than most players expect (#23). A game that says "Japanese = healthy" is wrong in the way R12 warns about.
6. **Cross-cultural transfer is unproven.** Every guideline above is written for its own population; nobody has tested "a Mexican plate for a Korean eater". Attribute each plate to its guideline and its person.
7. **No medical, allergy or condition advice** (#35, #36). Diabetes, kidney disease (potassium in kimchi), pregnancy, coeliac — all out of scope; a character says "ask your clinician" once, kindly, and never again.
8. **Licences:** an embedded OFF subset is share-alike (#29) and merging it with FDC makes the merged table ODbL; Mexico's national table has no licence (#33); Korea's book is non-commercial (#31); CREA asks to be cited and offers no open licence (#32). FDC and MEXT are the two clean ones.
9. **Every `[WEB]` claim here reached me as a search extract.** Nothing in this lens should be published outside the repo until the ~40 numbers are read off their pages.

## 3 · THE GOOD — the three things the evidence most supports doing

1. **Make the game about *planning and cooking dinner at home, for someone*, and let the pantry be a shopping list and a freezer.** That is #7, #8, #9, #10 and #11 in one loop, it is what all four guideline systems ask for, and its outcome — cooking confidence — is the one with ten-year longitudinal support. ## 1. FINDINGS — gamification psychology for a solo, calm, self-help eating game

**F1. Autonomous motivation predicts whether a health habit lasts; controlled motivation (points, pressure, "should") predicts whether it starts and then stops.** Ng et al.'s SDT systematic review found autonomy support from a provider raised autonomous motivation across physical activity, healthy eating, smoking, adherence, and autonomous motivation predicted long-term maintenance "far better than controlled motivation." A 2026 SDT-informed wellness framework maps autonomy to "user-defined goals with structured guidance," competence to "adaptive challenges," relatedness to "collaboration-focused progress sharing," and warns ranking systems get read "as forms of surveillance rather than motivation." `[WEB]` https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3441783/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42460595/ · https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1634267/full
**For this game:** she picks the dish, the goal and the pace. Nothing is assigned. This is R2's "expressive" dial (`GENRE-RULES.md`) with the psychology behind it.

**F2. Expected tangible rewards for an activity reduce the wish to do it unrewarded; informational feedback and unexpected rewards do not.** Deci, Koestner & Ryan's 128-study meta-analysis: engagement-, completion- and performance-contingent rewards undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation at d = −0.40, −0.36, −0.28. The same literature finds verbal/informational feedback does not undermine and can enhance. `[WEB]` https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf · https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543071001001 (the verbal-feedback and unexpected-reward distinction is `[TRAINING]` beyond what the extract stated).
**For this game:** no points for cooking. A character's remark about the dish is feedback, not payment; an unannounced gift from a neighbour is fine; a "claim your reward" ladder is the thing to refuse. This is the evidence under R6 ("the decoy is collection"), which was `[TRAINING]` in the last sweep and is now `[WEB]`.

**F3. The gamification-for-health evidence base leans positive but is thin, mixed, and mostly about points/badges/leaderboards and steps.** Johnson et al. 2016: 19 empirical papers, 59% positive, 41% mixed, evidence "mostly of moderate or lower quality," strongest for physical activity. Koivisto & Hamari 2019 (819 studies): results "lean towards positive" but "the amount of mixed results is remarkable"; education, health, and PBL "persist as the most common contexts." `[WEB]` https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214782916300380 · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0268401217305169
**For this game:** a calm, non-PBL, cooking-and-pantry game is essentially untested territory. Say so when the owner asks "does this work."

**F4. In randomized trials, gamification moves step counts modestly and moves diet not at all.** Mazeas 2022 (16 RCTs, 2,407 people): Hedges g = 0.42 during the intervention, g = 0.15 (95% CI 0.07–0.23) at follow-up averaging 14 weeks. Nishi/Kavanagh 2024 (36 RCTs, gamified vs non-gamified apps, ≥ 8 weeks): "No significant effects were observed on blood pressure, lipids, glycemic control, and dietary factors." `[WEB]` https://www.jmir.org/2022/1/e26779 · https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(24)00377-8/fulltext
**For this game:** the game layer cannot honestly claim to change what she eats. What it can claim is that she cooked, planned, and learned (F13–F14).

**F5. The one nutrition-app RCT with game-like "process motivators" that I found moved vegetables, but with a wait-list control and self-report.** Vegethon (n = 135 overweight adults, Stanford): goal-setting, self-monitoring, feedback plus "fun, surprise, choice, control, social comparison, competition." At 8 weeks: +0.7 servings/day vs −1.7 in control, difference +2.4 servings/day, p = 0.04; the dose–response with app use was "weak." `[WEB]` https://ijbnpa.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12966-017-0563-2 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28915825/
**For this game:** goal, self-monitoring, feedback are the working parts. Note "surprise" and "choice" sit on the supported side and "competition" is the part a solo game drops anyway.

**F6. Streaks retain through loss aversion and break through the same door.** Duolingo-attributed figures (secondary/vendor, unread primary): users with 7+ day streaks retain at 2.4× and Streak Freeze cut churn 21% among at-risk users. The mechanism of harm is the abstinence-violation effect — one miss reads as total failure and the longer the streak the harder the quit. `[WEB]` https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth · https://apptitude.io/blog/how-duolingos-streak-mechanic-actually-works/ (vendor) · https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/stigma-addiction-and-mental-health/202309/the-abstinence-violation-effect-and-overcoming-it. **One number I refuse to carry:** a "78% of users who reach a 14-day streak quit within 72 hours of a miss (UCSF)" figure circulates on a product-marketing page (https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/...) with no traceable study; treat it as unsourced.
**For this game:** if anything is counted, count upward ("42 dinners cooked") and never reset. Finch's design is the shipped example: it "sends only encouragement" after an absence and "actively deters you from an all-or-nothing mindset." `[WEB]` https://www.aviewfromthecave.com/what-is-finch-app/ · https://slate.com/technology/2026/09/finch-app-self-care-wellness-review.html. This is R11 ("no punishment for stepping away") extended from the session to the week.

**F7. After a lapse, self-kindness protects the next hour; guilt does not prevent the next lapse either way.** EMA study of 140 people in a weight-loss trial (6 prompts/day, 7 days): self-compassion after a dietary lapse was associated with less negative affect and greater perceived self-control afterwards; none of it predicted fewer lapses that day. `[WEB]` https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37619622/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8451927/
**For this game:** the character's line after a missed week is kind and practical ("the rice is still there"), not a verdict. Cheap: it is one string per character in two languages.

**F8. Leaderboards and social comparison harm the people a self-help game is for.** In leaderboard-only settings 31.3% reported negative psychological effects and 43.8% made upward comparisons that "amplif[ied] feelings of inadequacy"; macro leaderboards accumulate "perceptions of repeated failure" for lower performers. Nutrition-app users in a best-worst-scaling study ranked goals, progress bars and rewards highest and "leaderboards, narratives, social interaction, and badges" lowest. Food journalers told Cordeiro et al. they hid tracking from friends and colleagues. `[WEB]` https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1794299/full · https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/20552076241260482 · https://www.washington.edu/news/2015/04/16/research-identifies-blockers-in-tracking-meals-and-what-foodies-want/ (see corrected URL in Sources). Counterweight, honestly: STEP UP (JAMA IM 2019, ~600 adults) found the *competitive* arm best for steps (+920/day) and the only arm that held at follow-up. `[WEB]` https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2749761
**For this game:** no leaderboard, no feed. It is one person's pantry. The STEP UP result is about steps among strangers and does not transfer to a solo kitchen.

**F9. Relatedness can come from a character, and that is the channel a solo game has.** Bickmore's RCT (n = 263 older adults, embodied conversational agent vs pedometer; pilot: daily agent use for two months produced "significantly more walking") is the primary evidence that a relational agent sustains a health behaviour; Finch's whole retention thesis is "people will reliably do [healthy things] for someone, or something, that depends on them"; Animal Crossing players in lockdown described the game satisfying autonomy, competence and relatedness. `[WEB]` https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24001030/ · https://www.aviewfromthecave.com/what-is-finch-app/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9022176/ — Bickmore's 12-month result I could not read; `[TRAINING]` says the effect faded by 12 months, unverified.
**For this game:** the cast is the mechanic. A neighbour who remembers what she cooked last week and asks how it went is worth more than any counter — and Finch's own reviewers name the trap: the pet "can start to feel like one more thing to tend in month three."

**F10. Most health apps are abandoned within weeks, and the reasons are design, not willpower.** Scoping review (18 studies, 525,824 participants): abandonment is curvilinear, sharp early, "a median of 70% of users discontinuing use within the first 100 days"; 22 reasons in six themes (technical, privacy, poor UX, content, time/cost, evolving goals); the fix named is onboarding and personalisation. Cordeiro (141 journalers, 5,526 forum posts): apps "made me too focused and obsessive," designs "that create feelings of judgment can be self-defeating." Fitness-app day-30 retention of "3–4%" is a vendor figure. `[WEB]` https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26894233/ · https://sahha.ai/blog/health-app-churn-retention/ (vendor)
**For this game:** design for the return, not the streak; nothing red, no calorie ledger; a first session that ends with one real dish on the plan.

**F11. The novelty effect is real and has a measured shape.** Rodrigues et al.'s longitudinal study: gamification's effect began falling after four weeks, dipped for two to six weeks, then partially recovered between weeks six and ten ("familiarization effect"). `[WEB]` https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1203194/ · https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jcal.12385
**For this game:** the mechanic must be about the food, not about the mechanic, so week five still has something to do. That is R7 ("deciding, not performing") with a date on it.

**F12. Habits take two to five months, not 21 days, and self-chosen ones are stronger.** Singh et al. 2024 (systematic review, health habits incl. eight diet studies): medians 59–66 days, means 106–154, range 4–335; habit strength rose with "individual choice, affective judgements … and preparatory habits," morning practices strongest. `[WEB]` https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/12/23/2488 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39685110/
**For this game:** the horizon is a season, not a sprint — which the `SEASONS` idea in the earlier sweep already fits. "Preparatory habits" is the pantry: stocking, planning and thawing are the habit, cooking is the payoff.

**F13. An if-then plan roughly doubles follow-through; a meal plan is an if-then plan.** Gollwitzer & Sheeran meta-analysis, 94 studies, 8,000+ participants: d = 0.65 on goal attainment, d = 0.61 on "getting started." `[WEB]` https://www.researchgate.net/publication/37367696 · https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/goal_intent_attain.pdf
**For this game:** "Thursday, if I'm home by seven, the kimchi fried rice" is the single most evidence-backed thing the game can ask her to write, and it needs no clock and no score.

**F14. The behaviours the owner named — plan meals, keep a pantry, cook at home, learn recipes — are the ones that actually correlate with diet quality.** NutriNet-Santé (n = 40,554): 57% plan meals at least occasionally; planners had higher guideline adherence, more food variety, lower overweight/obesity odds. NHANES (n = 8,668): cooking dinner more often → higher HEI-2015. Cooking-skills interventions raise fruit/vegetable intake and cooking confidence, "certainty of evidence is low or very low," no BMI effect. `[WEB]` https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12966-017-0461-7 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31918785/ · https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0355940 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40795-019-0293-8 — all observational or low-certainty; correlation, not cause.
**For this game:** the game is scaffolding for those four behaviours. That is the honest pitch, and it is enough.

**F15. Autonomy-supportive wording is safe, cheap, and not a lever on its own.** Theory says "should/must" provokes reactance; the one digital 2×2 experiment I found (n = 526, vegetable intake) found no difference between framings — people rated it positively either way. `[WEB]` https://www.jmir.org/2019/10/e14074/ · https://doi.org/10.1177/2055207619832767
**For this game:** write kindly in both languages because it costs nothing; do not expect wording alone to carry retention.

**F16. Calm play has a measured calming effect; feeling compelled to play cancels it.** Johannes, Vuorre & Przybylski (2,700+ Animal Crossing and ~500 PvZ players, telemetry-linked): a small positive relation between play time and well-being, with enjoyment and need satisfaction mattering more than hours. Flower vs a 20-minute body-scan meditation (n = 80): heart rate and blood pressure fell equally; meditation won on self-reported stress. `[WEB]` https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33972879/ · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43076-021-00062-6
**For this game:** twenty minutes in the town is a legitimate wellbeing claim ("this is calming"); "you must log in daily" would be the thing that turns it off. Note the well-being study is correlational.

**F17. "Meaningful gamification" is the framework that matches R1–R5, and it names when rewards are acceptable.** Nicholson's RECIPE: Reflection, Exposition, Choice, Information, Play, Engagement; reward-based gamification "can be useful for short-term goals and situations where the participants have no personal connections or intrinsic motivation," but "rewards can reduce intrinsic motivation and the long-term desire to engage." `[WEB]` https://scottnicholson.com/pubs/recipepreprint.pdf
**For this game:** Information = nutrition facts with their sources on the card (the owner's "places to check"); Exposition = the cast; Reflection = "who taught you this one?" (R12); Play = a wrong dish adds dialogue (R2).

**F18. Serious games teach nutrition knowledge reliably and change behaviour unreliably.** Reviews 2018–2022: "immediate post-play improvements were common, particularly for knowledge and self-reported diet," while "durable change and objective transfer to real-world behaviour were less consistent"; dietary-habit improvements "were not significant." `[WEB]` https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9493252/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10056209/
**For this game:** "learn about nutrition" is a claim the evidence backs. "Eat better" is not.

## 2. LIMITATIONS

**L1. There is no trial of a calm or cozy game for eating.** Every RCT above is steps-and-badges or a nutrition *app*; the cozy-game evidence is correlational well-being (F16) and one 20-minute physiology study. The game's design rests on transfer, and the transfer is an argument, not a measurement.
**L2. Gamified vs non-gamified apps show no diet effect in 36 RCTs (F4).** The game may not claim it improves eating. It can claim it helps plan, cook, and learn (F13, F14, F18).
**L3. Calorie tracking is where a food game does harm.** Among 105 people with an eating disorder, ~75% used MyFitnessPal and 73% of those said it contributed to their illness; Cordeiro's journalers called apps obsessive and judgmental. `[WEB]` https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28843591/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26894233/. **Do not build a calorie or weight ledger.** If nutrition facts are shown, show them on the recipe card as information with a source, never as a daily total against a target.
**L4. Claims are regulated.** The FTC requires a "reasonable basis" for health claims and has acted against a weight-loss programme that could not substantiate "members lost an average of 53 pounds." FDA's 2026 General Wellness policy keeps software outside device regulation only if it is "intended solely for … maintaining or encouraging a healthy lifestyle" with no disease-specific claim. `[WEB]` https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance · https://www.insideprivacy.com/advertising-marketing/ftc-targets-weight-loss-membership-program/ · https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices (summaries via https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products). **The game must not promise weight loss, must not mention a disease, must not diagnose.** "Cooking at home is associated with better diet quality" is a permitted general-wellness sentence; "this game helps you lose weight" is not.
**L5. Allergies: I found no evidence either way, and that is the finding.** Nothing in this sweep supports a game computing whether a dish is safe for a person. A recipe card may carry the allergen note its *source* carries, attributed; the game must never be the allergen check. `[TRAINING]`, and I did not search the liability literature.
**L6. The vendor numbers are unverifiable.** Duolingo's 2.4× and 21%, Sahha's 3–4%, Finch's retention claims — all from company posts or secondary blogs I could not open. Use them as direction, not as figures.
**L7. Most nutrition-app trials use wait-list controls and self-reported food-frequency questionnaires (F5),** which inflate effects; the cohort studies (F14) are cross-sectional.
**L8. The novelty dip at weeks 4–10 (F11) will happen to this game too;** the only defence is content that is about the food.
**L9. Bilingual and Spanish-language evidence: not checked (see §5).**

## 3. THE GOOD — the three things the evidence most supports

1. **Let her choose, and make the choosing the game.** Autonomous motivation is the one predictor of maintenance (F1); self-selected habits are stronger (F12); need satisfaction, not hours, predicts well-being from play (F16). In engine terms this is R2 — the dish is the reply — and it is the cheapest option in A11.
2. **Make the plan and the pantry the mechanic, not the cooking.** An if-then plan is d = 0.65 (F13); meal planning and home cooking are the behaviours that correlate with diet quality (F14); "preparatory habits" raise habit strength (F12). The week's menu and the shelf are the behaviour-change techniques wearing pixel clothes, and they need no clock. This is the one place the missing inventory (F19) would earn its cost.
3. **A character who remembers, is kind after a lapse, and never counts down.** Informational feedback does not undermine (F2); self-kindness after a lapse protects the next hour (F7); a relational agent sustains a behaviour (F9); resetting streaks and judgmental numbers are the named reasons people quit (F6, F10). Two strings per character, in EN and ES, per lapse length — nearly free.

## 1 · FINDINGS

### A. Pantry and meal-planning apps — what they do, what users say

**1. Manual entry is the named cause of death for pantry apps, and the failure is drift, not effort.** Three pantry vendors, competing with each other, say the same thing: "the graveyard of pantry apps has one common cause of death: manual entry"; the cycle is 40 items on day one, five on day two, forgotten by week two, and by week three "every recipe suggestion is based on food you no longer have… the app becomes worse than useless: it is actively giving you wrong information"; "anything over 60 seconds to log a weekly shop dies." [WEB] (v, sx) https://www.pantrypersona.com/blog/best-pantry-inventory-apps-2026 · https://fango.fi/en/blog/best-pantry-inventory-app/ · https://recipyapp.com/blog/best-pantry-tracking-apps-2026. **For this game:** the pantry can never be a ledger the player maintains — that is R9's chore test failed on day three. A one-person pantry logged **by exception** (what ran out, what came in this week, said to a character in one line) is the only shape that survives; and a pantry that is *wrong* must be harmless (the game suggests, never asserts).

**2. The intake "fixes" — barcodes, receipts, loyalty cards — all have complaints, and none is available to a static offline page.** Pantry Check: "logging a full weekly shop means scanning each item's barcode individually… that's a lot of beeps," iPhone-only in 2026, ~200-item free cap, inventory "lives in the cloud behind an account," product identification inaccurate. NoWaste: "recurring issues with barcode scanning accuracy, synchronization problems, and occasional app crashes." Cooklist links grocery loyalty cards and receipts, and "it takes so long for receipts to come through that it sometimes thinks you don't have the groceries until the produce is expired." [WEB] (sx) https://fango.fi/en/blog/pantry-check-app-alternatives/ · https://thesurvivalmom.com/best-food-storage-app/ · https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nowaste-food-inventory-list/id926211004?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone · https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1352600944/cooklist-pantry-to-recipes/reviews. **For this game:** no server means no loyalty-card sync and no receipt OCR; barcode scanning is technically possible in a browser but needs a product database (Open Food Facts, share-alike licence — finding 27) — **[TRAINING]** for the browser-feasibility part. ### B. Recipe import and copy-paste — what a static offline game can and cannot do

**10. schema.org Recipe is the shared wire format, and its property names are verified against the schema source.** `recipeIngredient` ("free text or more structured values"), `recipeInstructions`, `recipeYield`, `prepTime`/`cookTime`/`totalTime` (ISO-8601 durations, e.g. `PT5M`), `nutrition` → `NutritionInformation` (`calories`…), `recipeCuisine`, `suitableForDiet`. [WEB] (read) https://raw.githubusercontent.com/schemaorg/schemaorg/main/data/schema.ttl (grep `:recipeIngredient` etc.) · https://github.com/micahcochran/scrape-schema-recipe (read, for the JSON-LD example and `PT5M`). **For this game:** a JSON-LD Recipe reader is ~100 lines of client JS and maps straight onto the reader's existing section types (finding 17). The *parse* is the easy half.

**11. Every serious importer works the same way: JSON-LD first, then Microdata/RDFa/OpenGraph, then site-specific HTML — and the reference library has 639 registered hosts and a generic schema.org fallback for the rest.** `recipe-scrapers` (Python): "If the 'supported_only' flag is enabled (the default), then only websites that are known to be supported… will return scrapers. When disabled, the library will attempt to retrieve generic schema.org recipe metadata from the HTML." Registered: giallozafferano, cookpad, justonecookbook, maangchi, directoalpaladar, cucchiaio. **Not registered: kiwilimon, 10000recipe (만개의레시피), mexicoinmykitchen, recetasgratis** — those would depend on the generic schema.org path, which I could not test (proxy). [WEB] (read) https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hhursev/recipe-scrapers/main/recipe_scrapers/__init__.py (`def scrape_html`, `supported_only`; 639 `.host()` entries) · https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hhursev/recipe-scrapers/main/recipe_scrapers/_factory.py (`SchemaScraperFactory`). **For this game:** the Italian and Japanese/Korean-diaspora English sites are well-trodden; the Mexican Spanish-language sites are the ones to test first, by hand, before promising anything.

**12. The fetch, not the parse, is the wall: a page on GitHub Pages cannot read another site's HTML.** MDN: "browsers restrict cross-origin HTTP requests initiated from scripts. For example, `fetch()` and `XMLHttpRequest` follow the same-origin policy… unless the response from other origins includes the right CORS headers." A `no-cors` fetch returns an opaque response with an unreadable body. [WEB] (read) https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/http/guides/cors/index.md · (sx) https://developer.okta.com/blog/2021/08/02/fix-common-problems-cors. **For this game:** "paste a URL and we fetch it" is **impossible** without a server, full stop. The three honest routes: (a) **paste the text** (ingredients + steps, or the raw `<script type="application/ld+json">` block) — works everywhere, today; (b) **a bookmarklet** that runs *on the recipe page*, reads its JSON-LD and hands it to the game via clipboard or a `#hash` — Paprika ships exactly this ("click the Paprika bookmarklet… a window will pop up… the parsed recipe"); (c) **PWA `share_target`** (finding 13). [WEB] (sx) https://www.paprikaapp.com/bookmarklet/ (v) · https://eathealthy365.com/the-ultimate-2026-guide-to-importing-recipes-into-paprika/.

**13. `share_target` (share a recipe page *to* the installed game) is Chrome-only: Chrome 89, Chrome Android 76; Firefox no; Safari and iOS no.** Verified against browser-compat-data; MDN's example maps shared `title`/`text`/`url` to query parameters on `GET`. [WEB] (read) https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/main/manifests/webapp/share_target.json · https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/progressive_web_apps/manifest/reference/share_target/index.md · (sx) https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=194593. `[CODE]` the game is already an installable PWA — `manifest.webmanifest` has `display: standalone` and no `share_target`; `sw.js` `CACHE = "mq-v158"` equals `GAMEV` in `content/meridian/config.js`. **For this game:** an Android-only nicety for iteration two; the shared `url` still cannot be fetched (finding 12), only the shared `text` is usable — so it is a paste with one fewer tap, not an importer.

**14. Cooklang is a plain-text recipe format a textarea can hold: `@ingredient{2%cups}`, `#pot`, `~{25%minutes}`, YAML front matter, `--` comments; the spec is MIT.** [WEB] (read) https://github.com/cooklang/spec · (sx) https://cooklang.org/docs/spec/. **For this game:** it gives "copy and paste some recipes" a grammar the reader can render and a shopping list can be summed from, with the person's own words as the recipe (finding 15). Whether to adopt it or a looser "one ingredient per line" heuristic is a design call — **[TRAINING]**: the looser one matches how people actually paste.

**15. An ingredient list is not copyrightable; the blogger's prose, headnote and photos are.** The Copyright Office's Circular 33 and the Compendium say "a mere listing of ingredients or contents is not copyrightable"; "the surrounding text… (the introduction, the headnotes, the variations, the stories) is fully copyrightable." [WEB] (sx — the PDF itself was blocked) https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf · https://copyrightalliance.org/are-recipes-cookbooks-protected-by-copyright/ · https://www.nycbar.org/reports/secret-ingredients-how-to-protect-recipes/. **For this game:** pasted recipes live in the player's `localStorage` and never in the public content pack; keep the source URL as the "person" (R12 attribution) and let the player write the steps in their own words. Anything *shipped* in the pack must be the household's own recipe or an institutional one (finding 21, Tsuji).

**16. Cookpad's "Cooksnap" is the one social mechanic worth stealing: a photo and "it was delicious" sent *to the recipe's author*, which also becomes your own log of what you cooked.** [WEB] (sx) https://cookpad.com/uk/cooksnap_intro (v) · https://blog.cookpad.com/uk/all-about-cooksnaps-cook-it-snap-it-share-it/ (v). Kiwilimón (México): 21,000 recipes, ingredient photos, MXN 45/month. [WEB] (sx) https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/recetas-kiwilim%C3%B3n/id680885433 (v). **For this game:** the "I made it" report to a *character* (the one who gave you the recipe) is the calm loop-closer — a reply, not a score (R2) — and it is the household's own history, which is what a cookbook actually is.

**17. `[CODE]` What we already have for this, re-grepped today.** The reader already renders a labelled blank (`s2.blank`, `engine/engine.js:3455`), a dropdown with option groups whose comment reads "the town's filter and sort" (`s2.sel`, `:3475`), a full form with text/password/area/select/checks (`s2.form`, `:3485`), and a button content wires (`s2.btn`). Copy-out exists (`navigator.clipboard.writeText` at `:3673`, `:3939`, `:4759`, `:4921`); **paste-in via `readText` does not** (grep: 0). `READS`/`DOCS` live in `content/meridian/docs.js:30`, `:91`. **Inventory: still 0 hits.** I did not find the pack-side `sel:` usage in `changarrito/content/docs.js` with a plain grep — the key may be spelled differently; re-grep before relying on it. **For this game:** the search we have is *find-in-this-text*, not a recipe index; a recipe index is a filter over an array with the `s2.sel` dropdown as its UI — which is the reader's existing pattern, not a new mechanism.

## 2 · LIMITATIONS — what the evidence does not support, what backfires, what must not be promised

- **No adult evidence that gamification changes diet** (finding 25). The children's evidence is school-delivered with parents enrolled (finding 24). A game may say "helps you plan and remember"; it may not say "helps you eat healthier."
- **No medical, weight-loss or allergy claims.** Nothing found supports a game as a source of truth for allergens; pasted recipes carry no reliable allergen data (schema.org `suitableForDiet` is optional and rarely filled — **[TRAINING]** for "rarely"). A game that lists "contains nuts" from a blogger's text is making a promise it cannot keep. Say "check the package" and mean it.
- **Computed nutrition on pasted recipes is unreliable** (Mealime's complaint, finding 5) and a computed *score* becomes a verdict on someone's food (Yuka, finding 8). Show sources, not numbers.
- **The pantry ledger backfires by week three** (finding 1); expiry lists become guilt lists; a 24-hour vanishing offer is scarcity (finding 5); a planned-meals calendar that marks missed days is a day budget (finding 4, R10).
- **URL import cannot exist without a server** (finding 12). Anyone who promises "paste a link" on GitHub Pages is promising a proxy. `share_target` is Chrome/Android only (finding 13).
- **Copyright**: ingredient lists are safe, prose and photos are not (finding 15). Nothing pasted may enter the public pack.
- **The "cozy" label is not the owner's "chill"** (finding 22); Kokoro Kitchen and Soup Pot are the closest to it and I could not verify either's reception first-hand.
- **Vendor sourcing**: the abandonment statistics (findings 1, 9) come from competing vendors; the 90%/30-day figure is unsourced.
- **This sweep found no calm Italian home-cooking game and did not search for Mexican cooking games beyond what the 2026-09-11 sweep already covered** (Tamales: Con Familia, Guacamelee).

## 3 · THE GOOD — the three things the evidence most supports doing

1. **Three offers today, from a person, that never vanish.** KptnCook and Mealime prove users thank you for fewer choices (finding 5); Squire's Quest proves the plan is the active ingredient (finding 24); Stardew/Spiritfarer prove "a person gives you the recipe" reads as warmth (finding 23). No clock, no scarcity, and it runs on `INTERVIEW` and a `READS` card with zero engine change (A11's cheapest row).
2. **Paste in, render, copy out — and the pantry only ever subtracts.** JSON-LD/plain-text paste into the reader (findings 10, 14, 17), the source URL kept as the attribution, the shopping list as recipe-minus-pantry (finding 3), the pantry logged by exception in one line to a character (finding 1). Everything here is client-side and already half-built (`tlArea`, `s2.form`, `s2.sel`, clipboard write).
3. **"I made it" as the reply, not the score — plus a citation card per cuisine.** Cooksnap's report-to-the-author (finding 16), Soup Pot's chorus (finding 18), Finch's bird that never dies (finding 26), and Kokoro Kitchen's "lateness costs nothing" (finding 19) are four shipped answers to A11 that are not "the player enjoys the freedom." The citation card (finding 27) is how "have places to check about this facts" becomes content instead of a number.

## 1 · FINDINGS

**Cross-cutting (the owner said "open to all")**

1. **There is one cuisine-neutral yardstick for "healthy", and it is small.** WHO: at least 400 g fruit and vegetables a day, under 5 g salt, free sugars under 10% of energy. `[WEB]` https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet — *For this game:* three numbers the town can show in EN/ES on a notice board, with the WHO link, and never more than that.

2. **All four countries publish an official native-language food guide, and they are the "places to check".** Japan: the Food Guide Spinning Top (MHLW + MAFF, five dish groups, grains on top, a person running on it for exercise). Korea: the Food Balance Wheels (Korean Nutrition Society, 2010, six groups plus a glass of water). Mexico: El Plato del Bien Comer in NOM-043-SSA2-2012 (three groups: verduras y frutas / cereales y tubérculos / leguminosas y alimentos de origen animal, water at the centre) and the Guías Alimentarias 2023 (SSA/INSP/UNICEF: ten recommendations, fresh-local-minimally-processed, less red meat, "respeto a la cultura alimentaria mexicana"). Italy: CREA Linee Guida 2018 (13 directives, "standard Italian portions", EVOO, whole grains, legumes). `[WEB]` https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/japan/en/ · https://www.maff.go.jp/j/syokuiku/guide/pdf/00_en_guide.pdf · https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/republic-of-korea/en/ · https://uo.edu.mx/plato-del-bien-comer-la-guia-alimentario-de-mexico/ · https://www.gob.mx/salud/sinsamac/documentos/guias-alimentarias-saludables-y-sostenibles-para-la-poblacion-mexicana-2025 · https://ve.scielo.org/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0798-07522023000200102 · https://www.crea.gov.it/en/web/alimenti-e-nutrizione/-/linee-guida-per-una-sana-alimentazione-2018 — *For this game:* each cuisine's kitchen links its own government's guide, in its own language. Institutional, not vendor.

3. **UNESCO lists three of the four as practices, not dishes.** Traditional Mexican cuisine (2010, "the Michoacán paradigm": milpa, chinampas, nixtamalization, molcajete/comal-type utensils, "collectives of female cooks"); the Mediterranean diet (2010, expanded 2013 to seven countries — "particularly the sharing and consumption of food"); washoku (2013, "respect for nature", New Year); kimjang (2013, "making and sharing kimchi", methods "transmitted from a mother-in-law to her newly married daughter-in-law"). `[WEB]` https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-mexican-cuisine-ancestral-ongoing-community-culture-the-michoacan-paradigm-00400 · https://ich.unesco.org/en/decisions/5.COM/6.41 · https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/washoku-traditional-dietary-cultures-of-the-japanese-notably-for-the-celebration-of-new-year-00869 · https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/kimjang-making-and-sharing-kimchi-in-the-republic-of-korea-00881 — *For this game:* the heritage bodies define each cuisine by a social act between named people, which is R12 handed to us: the mole belongs to Doña Rosa, the kimchi to somebody's mother-in-law.

**Japanese**

4. **Ichiju-sansai is a slot template, not a recipe: rice + soup + one main + two vegetable sides.** Six hundred years old (honzen ryōri, Muromachi), and today "a flexible framework for traditional and modern Japanese cuisine, Asian and Western cuisines, and beyond." `[WEB]` https://www.justonecookbook.com/ichiju-sansai/ (blog, ads) · https://www.nippon.com/en/guide-to-japan/gu020011/ · https://www.japanculinaryinstitute.com/post/japanese-home-cooking-one-soup-three-side-dishes — *For this game:* a meal planner is five empty slots. Filling them is a fit puzzle (R3) with no clock and no inventory needed — one shelf, not a house.

5. **Sa-shi-su-se-so is an ordering rule with a physical reason.** Sugar (sa) → salt (shi) → vinegar (su) → soy (se) → miso (so): "Sugar does not penetrate into the ingredients as fast as others"; soy "cooking too long dulls its flavor and aroma"; miso goes in "off the heat at the very end." `[WEB]` https://www.justonecookbook.com/sa-shi-su-se-so-seasoning-order/ — *For this game:* the prior sweep's mole finding generalises — order-of-operations is a puzzle that needs no timer (R1/R7), and the wrong order produces a real, duller dish rather than a fail state.

6. **The beginner pantry is four things.** "Rice, soy sauce, miso, and dashi. These four alone let you make miso soup, rice bowls, and simple glazed proteins"; then sake, mirin, vinegar, sugar, kombu, katsuobushi. `[WEB]` https://www.justonecookbook.com/categories/pantry/pantry-essentials/ · https://tomatostoreus.com/blogs/tomato-japanese-grocery-pantry-blog/japanese-pantry-essentials-beginners (vendor) — *For this game:* the first shelf of the Japanese kitchen is four sprites.

7. **First five, as the biggest English-language Japanese home-cooking site teaches them:** miso soup with dashi from scratch (10 minutes, one pot), onigiri, teriyaki salmon/chicken, a donburi, then nimono — "Once you master dashi, dishes like miso soup, nimono (simmered vegetables), and sukiyaki become truly special." `[WEB]` https://www.justonecookbook.com/homemade-miso-soup/ · https://www.justonecookbook.com/everyday/quick-and-easy/ — *For this game:* a technique ladder (dashi → soup → simmer), which is progression with no numbers.

8. **The Japanese diet's health evidence is real and observational.** Meta-analysis of prospective cohorts: pooled RR 0.83 for CVD mortality, 0.80 stroke, 0.81 heart disease at high adherence; Ohsaki cohort: highest adherence associated with reduced all-cause mortality, functional disability and dementia; JACC cohort concurs. `[WEB]` https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9147868/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9146082/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13487901/ — *For this game:* "associated with", never "prevents".

9. **Misconception: Japanese food is not low-salt.** Mean intake 10.1 g/day (2019 National Health and Nutrition Survey), double WHO; "soy sauce, soybean paste, and salted fish account for 44% of total salt intake, and seasonings overall contribute 66%"; national target 7 g by 2032; "Low sodium soy sauce is generally not available in Japan"; the same meta-analysis found "increased salt consumption elevated the risk for cardiovascular disease and stroke mortality." `[WEB]` https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9899568/ · https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250218/From-soy-sauce-to-sodium-labels-How-Japan-is-rethinking-salt-reduction.aspx · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9147868/ — *For this game:* the soup is where the salt hides; the Japanese kitchen's one health line is about the soy-and-miso spoon, not about fat.

**Korean**

10. **The bapsang is abundance by design: rice + soup + kimchi (always) + banchan, counted 3, 5, 7, 9, 12-cheop.** "Kimchi isn't counted in the cheop system because it's considered so essential it exists in its own category"; odd numbers outside royal cuisine. `[WEB]` https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banchan · https://www.tastekoreanfood.com/ktrend/guide-to-banchan-korean-side-dishes (vendor) — *For this game:* banchan are made ahead and eaten across days — the R8 "abundance" third, and a 3-cheop → 5-cheop growth that is progression without a score.

11. **The K-diet's healthy pattern is namul and jang, not barbecue.** "Proportionally high consumption of fresh or cooked vegetables (Namul), moderate to high consumption of legumes and fish and low consumption of red meat"; fermentation the representative method; banchan "mostly seasoned with various jang… and sesame or perilla oil." `[WEB]` https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352618116300099 (Journal of Ethnic Foods, 2016) — *For this game:* the Korean kitchen's verb is "season a vegetable", which is a cheap, repeatable, un-timed act.

12. **Five jars, then five dishes.** Buy first: ganjang, gochujang, doenjang, sesame oil, ssamjang (plus gochugaru). First dishes: doenjang-jjigae ("Koreans' everyday house food"), kimchi-jjigae, a namul, bibimbap, and kimchi itself as the long project. `[WEB]` https://www.myfreshdash.com/post/the-ultimate-korean-sauce-guide (vendor) · https://kimchimari.com/basic-korean-pantry-list-of-essentials/ (blog) · https://www.maangchi.com/recipe/doenjang-jjigae · https://www.maangchi.com/recipes/easy — the *ordering* of the five is `[TRAINING]`; Maangchi's site was not readable.

13. **Korean evidence is thinner than Mediterranean, and Korean researchers say so.** "A lot of clinical trial data related to the Mediterranean diet have been accumulated… but K-Food is still under interventional trial"; reported benefits are body weight and gastrointestinal; hansik associated with lower metabolic-syndrome risk. `[WEB]` https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s42779-023-00184-5 · https://journalofethnicfoods.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s42779-023-00172-9 — *For this game:* do not borrow Mediterranean-strength language for Korean food.

14. **Caveat: kimchi is about 20% of Korean sodium, and salted/pickled intake has a gastric-cancer signal in case-control studies that cohorts do not confirm.** Meta-analysis: +15% gastric cancer risk per 40 g/day pickled vegetables; a 2.2 M-person cohort: 1.1× with salt preference; but "cohort studies… showed no clear association." National sodium 3,136 mg/day in 2023 (down from 3,289 in 2019, still above WHO's 2,000); top sources noodles, dumplings, kimchi; the state now frames low-sodium cooking as heritage. `[WEB]` https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3204471/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7225928/ · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0002916523018154 · https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10521873 · https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/health/20250701/noodles-dumplings-kimchi-among-top-sources-of-sodium-in-koreans-diets · https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/health/20260829/less-salt-more-soul-korea-blends-heritage-health-at-national-museum — *For this game:* neither "superfood" nor "danger"; a less-salt kimchi is a Korean public-health message, so the game may say it without speaking over the culture.

**Mexican**

16. **Mexico's own 2023 guide tells you to cook traditional food.** Ten recommendations, "alimentos frescos, locales y mínimamente procesados", less red meat and embutidos, "respeto a la cultura alimentaria mexicana, fomentando el uso de ingredientes y recetas tradicionales." `[WEB]` https://ve.scielo.org/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0798-07522023000200102 · https://www.nutrikit.mx/blogs/news/las-10-recomendaciones-de-las-guias-alimentarias-2023-para-una-dieta-saludable-y-sostenible (vendor) — *For this game:* the health message and the cultural message are the same message, in Spanish, from the state.

Pantry: dried beans, masa harina, dried chiles (ancho "the workhorse"), chipotles en adobo (Jinich: three cans a week), lime. `[WEB]` https://www.npr.org/2022/10/28/1132205161/mexican-cooking-ingredients-expert-tips · https://www.mexicoinmykitchen.com/basic-recipes/ · https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/pati-jinich-just-told-us-213444313.html

## 2 · LIMITATIONS

- **The evidence is observational for Japan and Mexico, thin for Korea, and contested-RCT for the Mediterranean.** The game may say "associated with lower risk in cohort studies (link)". It must never say prevents, treats, reverses, or cures; and never name a disease as an outcome the player is earning.
- **No weight loss, no calorie counting.** A 38-study review (Flinders) found regular diet/fitness-app users more likely to have problematic food and exercise habits; 73% of MyFitnessPal users with a diagnosed eating disorder felt the app contributed; one RCT in low-risk women found no harm in a month. `[WEB]` https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1074348 · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1471015317301484 · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212267221007346 — no weight field, no calorie meter, no streak on quantity eaten. A calm game that counts calories has rebuilt the pressure with extra steps (R4's trap).
- **Salt is the shared blind spot of the two Asian cuisines** (findings 9, 14) and the game must not present them as unconditionally healthy; the jar of soy sauce and the kimchi crock carry the one caveat.
- **Allergens cannot be promised.** FDA's nine: milk, egg, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, wheat, peanuts, soybeans, sesame (FASTER Act, labelled since 2023-01-01). `[WEB]` https://www.fda.gov/food/food-allergies/faster-act-sesame-ninth-major-food-allergen · https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/have-food-allergies-read-label. Soy, wheat, fish, sesame and shellfish are pervasive in JP/KR pantries (soy sauce, miso, dashi, sesame oil, jeotgal). The EU list is 14 items — `[TRAINING]`, not fetched. The game may say "contains" from the recipe's own text; never "safe for".
- **Kimchi is neither superfood nor danger** (finding 14). Don't sell fermentation as medicine.
- **Per-recipe nutrition numbers need a database the repo does not have.** USDA FoodData Central, Japan's Standard Tables of Food Composition, Korea's food composition DB and Mexico's INSP tables exist — `[TRAINING]`, none fetched. A number without one of these behind it is a made-up number.
- **Authenticity is the cultural trap, not inaccuracy** (prior sweep R12). Four "national" kitchens is exactly the museum; four households is the game.

## 3 · THE GOOD

1. **The meal structure is the mechanic, and each one is nationally endorsed.** Ichiju-sansai (1+1+1+2), the 3-cheop bapsang, the milpa plate, primo/secondo/contorno: slot templates a pantry fills, no clock, no inventory system needed for one shelf.
2. **Each cuisine has a native ordering rule or unrushable project that is R1/R4 for free.** Sa-shi-su-se-so; kimjang and the yearly cycle behind it; nixtamal and the twenty moles; Hazan's 45-minute sauce. The wrong order gives a real, worse dish; the wait forbids haste.
3. **Every one of the four countries' own guides says "cook the traditional food, fresh, less processed."** The health message and the cultural message are one message, from institutional, native-language sources — which is the owner's "have places to check about this facts" answered by the countries themselves.
