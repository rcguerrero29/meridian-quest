## §1 — How shipped games handle "the vehicle is not here yet"

**The question he was answering:** our trolley runs every 19 s and takes ~13 s to cross, so the worst
case wait at a stop is about 32 s. He said *acceptable* — and then asked whether anybody outside this
repo has a better answer. They do, and it does not agree with what we built.

### The finding that matters most: nobody makes you wait for the vehicle. They make waiting optional.

**Cyberpunk 2077, update 2.1 (Dec 2023) — the NCART metro.** CDPR shipped an actual ridable metro,
five lines, 19 stations. The design decision worth copying is not the train, it is the **fork at the
platform**: standing at a station you are offered *fast travel to your destination* **or** *ride the
line in real time* — and **once you are riding you can skip the rest of the sequence at any point.**
The immersive option and the instant option are the same button, and the immersive one is abandonable
mid-ride. `[WEB]` https://www.pcgamer.com/cyberpunk-2077-is-finally-getting-a-fully-functional-metro-system-so-you-can-ride-around-night-city-in-style/
and https://www.dexerto.com/cyberpunk-2077/cyberpunk-2077-ncart-metro-locations-how-to-use-2415546/

**Red Dead Redemption 2 — trains.** Two entirely separate systems wearing one costume. Buying a
ticket at a station *is* fast travel, instant. The train that physically rolls past is a **world
object you may board while it is moving or stopped**, and it is there for the ride and the robbery,
not for transport. Rockstar did not merge them, and did not make the ticket wait for the train.
`[WEB]` https://reddead.fandom.com/wiki/Trains

**Shenmue (1999) — the counter-example, and it is the famous one.** Real schedules, real waiting, no
way to pass time. The line quoted back at it for twenty-five years: *critics questioned why waiting
for buses on a sidewalk would be fun in a game when it is not fun in real life.* The specific failure
named in the retrospectives is not the wait — it is that **there was no means to fast-forward**, so
the player's only move was to do nothing.
`[WEB]` https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-shenmue-i-ii/ and https://gamecritics.com/brad-gallaway/shenmue-second-opinion/

**The general literature agrees.** *"In single player games, it is common for game time to pass
quickly when resting, waiting or using some sort of automatic travel, as there is little to be gained
from making a player do nothing while that happens."* The design guidance on fast travel is
consistent on one point above all others — **agency: the system should feel like a tool at the
player's disposal, not a forced mechanic.**
`[WEB]` https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_travel and https://www.designthegame.com/learning/tutorial/fast-travel-video-games

### What this says about our 32 seconds

| | Their answer | Ours today |
|---|---|---|
| Is waiting ever mandatory? | No — every one of them has an instant path | **Yes.** The tram is the only ride and it comes when it comes |
| Can you abandon the ride? | Cyberpunk: yes, mid-ride, any time | n/a — you cannot board at all |
| Is the wait *doing* anything? | Shenmue's is the cautionary tale precisely because it is not | Ours is not either |

**So: 32 s is acceptable *only because the trolley is not the way to get anywhere* in Meridian — the
two streets already touch through a door.** That is exactly his ruling in ③. The moment the trolley
becomes a route, the 32 s becomes the Shenmue problem and the answer from four shipped games is the
same: give the instant path, keep the ride as the thing you choose.

### And the number, for the part he did ask us to build

A tram standing at a stop is not a game invention and there is a measured answer. **Levinson's model,
the landmark one in transit engineering: dwell = 5 s + 2.75 s per boarding or alighting passenger.**
Observed means on real routes cluster at 12–16 s with heavy variance; door open-and-close alone is
reckoned at 2–5 s.
`[WEB]` https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1340&context=jpt and
https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/tcrp/docs/tcrp100/Part4.pdf

One passenger, one door: **≈ 7.75 s real.** Our `TRO_HOLD` is **1400 ms** and it is not a dwell at
all — it is the obstruction pause. *The tram in this game has never once stopped at a stop.*
That is the hole ③ asks us to fill.

## §2 — "What are best practices, especially with current functionality?"

**The question he was answering:** the door/occlusion one — *standing on things I'm standing behind*.
He is asking what the industry does, and pointedly, **what it does with what we already have**: a 2D
canvas with a painter's algorithm, three flat cameras, and one 3D renderer that already ghosts.

### The rule every 2D game in this shape follows, and the pitfall named in the same breath

**Y-sorting (the painter's algorithm on the depth axis).** *"In 2D games there is no Z-buffer… the
furthest objects are painted first and the closest objects are painted last."* In a top-down or
isometric view the **Y axis is depth**, so a lower Y draws before a higher Y — and that single rule is
what lets a sprite be behind a tree at one step and in front of it at the next.

The pitfall is stated as a beginner's error and it is **exactly our bug**: *"beginners often sort by
the y position of the sprite's top-left corner, but you must sort by the **feet** of the object."*
A tall thing sorted by its top is always "far away", so the player always draws on top of it — which
is the sentence *"im standing on things im standing behind"* written in code.
`[WEB]` https://itsfuad.medium.com/y-sorting-in-2d-games-2983dc745275

### And for the 3D camera, the thing we already half-have

**Baldur's Gate 3's occlusion cutout** is the current reference implementation, and it is a *local*
effect, not a global transparency: spherecast from the character toward the camera, and where it is
blocked, hand the hit point to the material and **open a soft noise-edged hole around just that
point.** The practitioner notes that matter for us:

- run the cast **on a delay** and interpolate, rather than every frame;
- **separate ground from walls** so you never fade the floor the player is standing on;
- keep a **shadow pass** so faded geometry still casts;
- and fade **gradually**, so a camera swing near a building is a transition and not a pop.

`[WEB]` https://80.lv/articles/artist-recreated-baldur-s-gate-3-occlusion-cutout-effect and
https://www.artstation.com/artwork/WXVnyD

**What that says about us, with current functionality:** `T3GHOST` at 0.68 is the *global* version of
this — the whole wall goes glassy. BG3's answer is that the hole should be **around the player**, not
around the building, and that the edge should be soft. We cannot spherecast in a canvas 2D camera,
but the ordering fix above is free and is the actual reported bug; the ghost is already shipped for 3D
and the open question is only whether it becomes local.

### The engine shape — Beto

- **One new state: `dwell`.** Four total. `hold` must outrank it (`:1175` tests `troAhead` first and
  unconditionally), or a car dwelling with a cat on the rails departs on its timer.
- **`TRO.t` is already overloaded twice** — headway clock in `away` (`:1172`), hold clock in `hold`
  (`:1176`). A third overload is exactly how *"do not send a new one"* gets violated by accident.
  Reset it on entry to `dwell`.
- **A second car is unrepresentable, not merely unlikely.** `TRO` at `:1151` is one object literal;
  `troUpdate` has exactly one statement that starts a run (`:1173`) and it is inside
  `if(TRO.state==="away")`. The owner's queue ruling is structurally guaranteed today and must stay so.
- **The stop list is a seam and this change forces it open.** `troAtStop` (`:1164-1166`) is a
  *predicate* — "is the player near a `Y`". Braking needs a *query* — "what is the next stop ahead of
  `TRO.x`". **You cannot build the second from the first at any price.**
- **`px,py` are integers; `fx,fy` are the floats.** `:388`. The camera (`:1565`, `:1682`), the 2D hero
  (`:1752`) and the 3D hero (`engine3d.js:806`, `:911`) all read `fx,fy`. So the ride lives in
  `fx,fy` and **`px` may never become fractional** — `sanitizeSave` rounds and clamps it (`:462`,
  `:498`), so a mid-ride save or a `#save=` link would corrupt silently.

### The three landmines, all verified

1. **`troAhead` deadlocks on the westbound line — and only the westbound line.** `:1160-1163` treats
   a passenger as a pedestrian on the rails. On `ex` (`dir=-1`, `nose=TRO.x`) a rider at
   `px=Math.round(TRO.x+0.5)` gives `d=-(1-r)`, inside the `[-0.6,2.6]` window whenever the
   fractional part `r>0.4` — so the car holds, `TRO.x` freezes, `r` never changes, **and it holds
   forever with the player inside it, in the middle of Calle Dos.** Eastbound on `st` the same rider
   is safe. *Same code, same rider, deadlocks on one line and not the other* — the asymmetric bug
   that ships because the demo was recorded on the wrong street.
2. **`moving=true` for a rider opens the fast-travel panel under a moving tram.** `:2888-2890` reads
   the tile under `px,py` at the end of a step and fires `openTravel()` on a `Y`. A rider's `px`
   sweeps the line and stops on the stop tile. Two bugs from one flag.
3. **`tryStep()` runs every frame the player is not `moving`** (`:2894`), gated only on `moving`,
   `held` and `warpT` — so **a rider who swipes walks off a moving tram in mid-air.**

### A live bug this run did not cause — the trolley runs over the dog

`troAhead` (`:1162-1163`) checks `w.npcs` and `CRIT`. **`DOG`, `CAT` and `PIG` are separate globals**
(`:1842`, `:1897`, `:1941`); `CRIT` is built at `:1993` from `CRITTERS` only. So Fred can stand on the
rails and the tram rolls through him. That is a direct violation of the settled ruling in
`docs/OWNER.md` — *"pedestrian, vehicle or even fauna… Braking for the hummingbird and running over
the pigeon is not a policy, it is a blind spot."* **Live today, before any of this work.** One line,
its own red test, its own PR.

### The numbers — Rigo, and he built them rather than felt them

He refused to quote Levinson at a toy and refused to guess. He rebuilt the real number out of the
physical acts — *door 2 s, one person up the steps 3–4 s, driver's check 1 s, door 2 s* = **8.5 s for
a single-door heritage car, one passenger, no fare** — said the word **compression**, halved it, and
named the floor that does not compress:

> **`TRO_DWELL = 4200` ms.** Under about **2500 ms a stop reads as a stutter, not a stop**, however
> short the line is. 4.2 s is the smallest number that still looks like a decision rather than a hitch.

And it is a **minimum, not a duration**: doors stay open while the player is still on or beside the
stop tile, to a hard cap of 10 000 ms, *"because a driver holds for somebody plainly coming, and stops
holding when it becomes obvious they are not"* — with the cap there so a player who walks away from
the keyboard cannot freeze the line.

Three more, each with its reason:

- **If nobody is waiting it does not stop** — it slows to 0.8 tiles/s from 1.5 tiles out, rolls
  through and one tile past, then accelerates back. *"That is exactly what a driver does at a flag
  stop with nobody on it: he slows, he looks, he goes."* And it is what teaches a player where the
  stop is, because the car acknowledges it on every pass whether or not anyone is boarding.
- **It stands flush, not centred** — `dir>0: TRO.x = x_stop`, `dir<0: TRO.x = x_stop−1` — so a
  passenger on the stop tile steps one tile sideways into the car. Checked against both maps:
  **centred would hang a tile off the west edge of `st`.**
- **Grace on departure.** Until the car's trailing end clears the stop tile (~1.5 s at creep speed) a
  player pressing toward it still gets on. *"A driver who catches you in the mirror running does stop
  again, once."* Being locked out for a whole headway over a half-second mistake is the meanest thing
  in the build.

### Rigo's #1, and it outranks everything else in this file

> **A tram that stops and never opens anything.**
>
> `engine/engine3d.js:594-631` builds a body, a skirt, a roof, four wheels, six side windows, two end
> windows and a driver. **There is no door. There is no step.** So the car will come in beautifully,
> brake beautifully, and stand beside you for 4.2 seconds with a sealed flank — and every one of
> those 4200 ms will read to a player as the game having hiccuped.
>
> **A stop is a door opening.** If nothing changes on the side facing the platform during the dwell,
> we have not built a stop. We have built a pause, and a pause is what we already have.

### 5 · Getting out — the exit already exists, and there is exactly one way to lose it

Every visible control at the stop, measured: **↻ 40×40 · ⚙ 38×38 · ⛶ 38×38 · 🗺 38×38 · 🐾 42×42.**
**Not one meets 44 px.** (Checked by the calling session against `index.html:75` `.gear` 38px and

Meanwhile the d-pad's arrows are **exactly 44×44**, and in drag mode the movement input is the whole

**The one way to lose it, at file:line.** `tryStep()` opens with `if(moving||!held)return;` and

### 7 · `troCome` loses its occasion, and two strings that must exist do not

`troCome` fires on `troAtStop() && !TRO.called` (`:1171`) — *i.e. you summoned it.* Whatever happens to
the summon, the sentence then has to cover two different moments and is only true for one. What has to
be said (Paty writes the EN and ES; Rosa says what it must MEAN):

- **A — approaching.** *On its way, nothing to press.* `troCome` can keep this job **if** it fires two
  seconds before arrival instead of on the same frame.
- **B — stopped, door open, clock running.** *It is here. Walk into it.* **Does not exist, and is the
  one string the feature cannot ship without.**
- **C — aboard.** *You are riding; step off whenever.* **Does not exist.** Without it, a person whose
  screen has just started scrolling on its own has been told nothing, and **the first thing they will
  do is fight the controls.**
- **D — departing without you.** Optional. *"I would rather have silence than a scold."*

called trolley — one you ride, one that is a menu.**
