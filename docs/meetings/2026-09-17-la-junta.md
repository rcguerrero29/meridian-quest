# La junta — the learning goal, what should guide us, and how the endings get revamped

*2026-09-17. The owner, in the session (`docs/ASKS.md`, last row, already logged): **"do we need to review the learning goal within the meridian open world concept?"** · **"what is recommended or what should guide us?"** · **"how do we revamp this, nacho has to give input too and check with the other agen[ts]"** — and the sentence the room was told to hold: **"doesnt have to be perfect, but tell the agents to ensure a person just meeting the characters cant be confused as to what they are talking about."***

*The room: **Nacho** (story), **Don Güero** (the city and its parcels), **Pili** (how things read), and one invited guest, **el que llega** — a first-time-player lens. Every position was cross-examined by a reader loyal to the code, not to the position. Nothing below is remembered: the tree is clean at `cf55b66`, `node test/smoke.js` returns `OK — 60 quests, maxXP 880, all invariants hold`, and every line reference in this document was opened again while writing it.*

*Words this readout uses, in plain language, because today's rule applies to the minutes too:*

- **`need`** — the number of quests a district counts before it plays its ending. La Espiga has eight quests and `need:5`, so it ends on the fifth one answered, whichever five those are.
- **tier / grade** — which of three ending paragraphs plays. Grade 3 = a flawless run, grade 2 = the middle, grade 1 = the rough one. **Tier 2 is what most people will read** (§1.3).
- **a seam** — a place in the shared engine where a pack (Meridian, the town, a future world) can declare something, instead of the engine hardcoding it.
- **the bump** — moving `GAMEV` in `content/meridian/config.js:3` and `CACHE` in `sw.js:6` together. The installed app serves files out of its own cache first; without the bump a change reaches brand-new installs only, and **never a returning player**.
- **a proxy guard** — a test that checks a stand-in for the thing it means (`docs/REGRESSION.md`).

---

## 0 · The answer in one paragraph, before the detail

**The learning goal is not broken and does not need re-deciding. The evidence for it is missing, and in two places the game actively says the opposite.** The open world is not the problem: the whole city crosses in 60 tiles, about 14 seconds of walking, and a good route spends 1.8 seconds walking per quest. What the open world costs is not time, it is **density** — 2,294 tiles you can stand on, 62 of them with anything in them. And the two places the game contradicts its own purpose are both cheap: it hands out its **top rank at 12 clean answers of 60** (`LEVELS=[0,45,90,120]`, `config.js:5`) and prints that rank as a **🏆 trophy over every ending paragraph** (`engine/engine.js:4434`), and it **never practises a single concept twice** — 89 labels across 60 quests, zero repeats, verified by exact string match. A gym where every exercise is done once and then retired is a tour, not a gym. The endings get revamped in **one pull request that contains the words, the engine seam and the guard together** — in that order inside the PR, for a reason the room proved: shipping the seam first would make the owner's own complaint louder in six districts, not quieter.

## 1 · Agenda 1 — "do we need to review the learning goal within the meridian open world concept?" `[CODE]`

### THE RECOMMENDATION

**No — do not re-open the goal. Re-open the curriculum's shape, and adopt one rule: *el segundo turno* — from the mercado on, no district may consist only of concepts the player has never met. At least one quest per district names a craft already practised somewhere else, and says so.**

The goal itself is written, in the code, and it is right: `repL.foot` in `content/meridian/strings.js` prints *"Generated by Meridian Quest — a practice gym, not a certificate."* Nothing about seven districts, two streets, a tram or a city plan contradicts that sentence. What contradicts it is the curriculum underneath:

1. **89 distinct concept labels over 60 quests, and not one is ever used twice.** Verified two ways — per choice (`choice.out.concept`, 315 choices, 287 carrying a concept) and per decision node. Zero collisions either way. There is no spiral, no callback, no second look.
2. **Two labels 48 quests apart are the same craft and are never connected**: `Grounding & refusal` (quest 0, HQ) and `Grounding` (quest 48, Nolasco). Nothing in the code forbids a repeat — the "zero repeats" fact is an accident of writing, not a rule anybody wrote.
3. **Tier 2 is the modal outcome by arithmetic, not by chance.** `gradeOf` (`engine/engine.js:500`) grades the first-try fraction of *answered* quests; at `need:5` grade 3 requires a flawless five. Every competent, imperfect player lands in tier 2 — **and tier 2 is the weakest writing in the game.** The best-read paragraph is the least-edited one.
4. **The first ten quests a player can reach teach in a different language from the other fifty.** Quests 0–9, all inside HQ: *RAG vs fine-tuning, Context windows & chunking, Model routing & cost design, PII/PHI governance, Evals & regression testing, Latency engineering.* The 44 quests in districts 2–7 carry almost no machine-learning vocabulary at all: *Reading the workaround, The honest exception, Who signs the rules.* The plain-language half is the better half and it is the half nobody reaches first.
5. **The intro still describes a game with one office in it.** `UI.en.in2` (`content/meridian/strings.js:5`): *"The office is a world. Roam it — hallways, the kitchen, the corner offices."* No street, no district, no tram, no map. And `in1` opens on *"The old lead **fine-tuned** something he shouldn't have"* — the jargon of quest 0, used before anything has taught it, on the very first screen. That is a direct hit on the rule the owner made today.

**Why this recommendation and not "reduce the open world":** because the measurement refutes the fear. A nearest-neighbour tour of every quest-giver in the city is ~430 tiles, about 103 seconds of walking, for all 60 quests and 88 decisions. The furthest tile from the start is 60 tiles away. The world is not stealing the practice's time. What it does do is spread 62 interactive tiles over 2,294 walkable ones, and three worlds are almost pure corridor: **`pk` (the park) is the third-largest world in the game with 200 walkable tiles and holds no person, no quest and no readable, and `PORTALS` has `pk → st` with no way back in except a dog leash**; `st` (Calle Principal) is the largest world in the game and carries one quest-giver; `f2` (the office) is the second-largest interior and has zero quests. That is a **density** ticket and a **content** ticket, not a reason to shrink the city.

**Why *el segundo turno* is the rule and not something grander:** it is the only change that makes the stated goal visible to the player inside the writing that already exists. A player who meets *Refusal by design* at Nolasco having already met *Grounding & refusal* at Meridian Labs is practising; a player who meets 89 things once is sightseeing. It costs nothing at plan time — no engine work, no new field — and `decisionReport()` already gathers the labels, so the return can be shown in the report the day the second label is written.

**One consequence adopted with it:** the **🏆** comes off the top of the ending panel (build order T3). A game that says *practice gym, not a certificate* must not print "AI LEGEND" over a story about a bakery when 48 quests remain. Verified cheap: `test/smoke.js:386` asserts the ending title **only** on the burnout path, so the non-burnout title has no guard on it at all.

## 2 · Agenda 2 — "what is recommended or what should guide us?" `[CODE]`

### THE RECOMMENDATION

**What guides us is a guard, not a style note. Adopt three rules in `docs/OWNER.md`, and accept the meta-rule that came out of this meeting: a rule no test can turn red is a preference. Two of the three can be turned red today; the third names its reviewer.**

**R1 · Nobody is introduced by a reference a newcomer cannot follow.** *(Already the rule, as of today. This meeting gives it a checkable definition.)* A line is legal if everything it names can be known at the earliest point a player can read it — and "earliest point" must be computed over **all three** gates the engine actually enforces, not just one:

- `qOpen` (`engine/engine.js:541`) — the district must be open (`c<=chSeen`);
- `pendingAt` (`engine/engine.js:735`) — a person only ever offers their own lowest unanswered quest, so order *within* one person is fixed;
- `GROWTH.staged` (`config.js:57`) — a building may not exist yet. The guest's example was corrected by the code here: Xochi's quest 15 is hard-gated behind Lupe's 12 and 13, because the `O` tile that is the only door into `lo` is laid by that staged growth. Anyone standing in front of Xochi has necessarily been to La Obra.

Without all three, the rule flags lines that are already safe, and the Spanish doubles the bill for fixing non-problems.

**R2 · An ending may only name what `need` guarantees.** `chClosed` (`engine/engine.js:475`) is `quests.filter(done).length >= need`. At `need:5` of 8, **three quests are always unanswered when the ending fires**. So `vepi2` (Velázquez, tier 2), which leans on Marta's paper, the map and the exception from quest 46, is written about an afternoon a default-path player never had. This one is a reviewer's rule, not a test — and it has a relief valve that already exists: `DOCS.velazquez.build` calls `dCalls(R,"velazquez")`, so detail moved into the district's document **disappears by itself** for a quest the player never answered. Evicted specifics do not fall into nowhere.

**R3 · No two districts may open the same way, and no ending may open on its own last quest's line.** This is the machine-checkable one and it is the one the room says is missing entirely. Today `test/smoke.js` asserts that each district's five ending strings **exist** in both languages (`:227`, `:1791`) and that `#epi` is **non-empty** (`:318`, `:341`). **Nothing anywhere asserts that two endings differ.** That is why six districts could ship the same five beats in the same order and CI never said a word.

### The meta-rule, which is the real answer to "what should guide us"

> **A rule that no test can turn red is a preference. Write it down as a preference, or write the test.**

This repo has already learned this shape one level down: `test/bump.js` exists because *"lockstep is not the same as movement"* — two strings that match is not the same as two strings that moved. **Sameness is the same kind of hole.** The absence of a rule against identical endings is not neutral; identical is the default, because a template is the cheapest thing to write.

Its immediate corollary, which the room found in the tree twice: **`docs/STORY.md` describes a design that was signed and never built** (the per-district letter, and the `ending:{mode:"doorstep"|"panel"|"quiet"}` seam — `CHAPTERS` carries `id, quests, need, epi, go, open, industry, role`, and no `mode`). `docs/OWNER.md:447` already says *"Signed ≠ built"*. This meeting adds the enforcement: **an unbuilt promise gets marked in the document that promises it, on the day it is found** — not in a meeting readout the next reader will not open.

## 3 · Agenda 3 — "how do we revamp this" (the endings) `[CODE]`

### THE RECOMMENDATION

**One pull request, containing the words, the engine seam and the guard together — and inside it, the words land before the seam. Not the seam first, not the words alone.**

Four specific calls inside that, each defended:

**3.1 · Drop "Saturday" as an opener in all 18 business-district paragraphs (and their 18 Spanish twins), but do not force a clock on all six.** All six open on the same word in both languages — and `espiga`'s Spanish opener is **byte-identical** to `mercado`'s (*"Sábado, hora de cerrar."*). The proposal that reached the room was "open on the hour the business runs on". The hours exist, but there are **five** of them, not six: mercado *seven*, **taller *seven*** (the same hour), espiga *four*, velázquez *ten*, nolasco *nine*, esquina *six-fifteen* — all verbatim in the burnout endings (`strings.js:190–221`). And "3:45" and "5:40" are **not** ending lines at all; they are quest titles (#34 Tito, #42 Karla). **So the rule is not "every ending opens on a clock" — it is "no two districts open the same way."** Taller in particular may not take six o'clock: `tepi1` already opens *"Saturday, six o'clock"* while its own last quest (#31, *The book*, `quests.en.js:347`) opens *"Six o'clock. The book's on the counter."* Taller opens on the object, not a time. **At least two districts must open on something that is neither a day nor an hour**, or the template has simply moved one slot to the right. One district has already done this correctly and can be the model: `esqepi1` opens *"The pot comes off the flame at 9:25."*

**3.2 · The template is upstream of the endings, and the fix is upstream too.** Six of seven districts' **last quests open with their own ending's day stamp and stage the same scene** — and espiga's are near-verbatim, which is issue **#208**. #208 is filed against La Espiga alone; it is structural and it lands in six districts. It is also **the default path, not an edge case**: the quest a writer built as the last visit sits eighth in an array of eight, `need` is five, so it is never among the required five.

**3.3 · Take the `finale:` seam — in the same PR as the words, never before them.** One line in `engine/engine.js:475` (`&& (c.finale==null || done.has(c.finale))`), six integers in `config.js`, one line in `test/smoke.js`. Built and run to green in a sandbox copy of this tree by the reader cross-examining Don Güero. It makes the district's last visit **guaranteed to be played** instead of optional. **And shipped on its own, with the strings unchanged, it makes the owner's complaint worse**: today a player who stops at `need:5` reads espiga's duplicated afternoon once, in the ending panel. With `finale:` live and `eepi1` untouched, **every** player in six districts reads quest #39's *"Saturday, closing. Two conchas on the last tray"* and then, immediately, the ending's *"Saturday, closing. Two conchas on the last tray, nothing in the merma bin"* — back to back, by construction. That is why the order inside the PR is load-bearing.

**3.4 · Fix the last district's dead end, and fix it with one word — not by deleting one.** `nolasco` and `esquina` **both** declare `open:"esquinaToast"` (`config.js:252` and `:265`), so finishing the final district of the game replays Meche's own introduction — *"a woman who has been on that corner since before the rails… forty messages she reads at four in the afternoon and nobody to hand them to"* — after you have spent four quests handing them to her. It is also the clearest live breach of today's rule: it opens *"there is a name on it you have walked past forty times."* The correct fix is `open:"endStayToast"` on the `esquina` entry (that string exists in both languages, `strings.js:192` / `:427`, and fires nowhere today). **Deleting the field and letting the engine's fallback fire fails CI** — see §6, contradiction 2.

### What was considered and *not* recommended

- **The letter (`la carta`).** `docs/STORY.md` promises *"the long version waits in your office as a letter, re-readable forever"*, with a grade asymmetry (short at grade 3, longest at grade 1). It does not exist, and neither does the seam the doc says is declared per district. **Parked, with its route named** (§8) — because the office form of it needs two engine capabilities nobody has built: a readable that is **hidden until earned** (a `READS` row has no visibility condition; `readMarks` and `readAt` show a tile whenever the document exists, which is why the six office posters already read *"Blank paper. Pinned up ready, nothing on it."* on a fresh save), and a **grade that stops moving** (`gradeOf` is recomputed live from `marks`, and nothing per-district is saved — so a player who closes the mercado at 5/5 and later answers the rest with one miss would find a *different* paragraph waiting; the payoff meant to be permanent would be mutable). Both have cheap routes. This week is not when.
- **The twelve burnout endings.** They are a Mad Lib (*"You learned on my floor / in my kitchen / in my van"*), and **Chelo and Tacho share the noun in both languages** (`floor`/`piso`). They are not unreachable but they are close: `STAKES={mode:"none"}`, so they only play after Settings → Admin mode → Hearts, per device. **One string per language changes** so two characters stop sharing a noun; the rest waits. The Spanish register mix (`tú` for four, `usted` for Licha and Meche) reads as deliberate for two older women addressing a stranger formally — it is not a defect and the room should stop calling it one.

## 7 · THE LEARNING

### 7.1 · La plantilla — the template trap *(named, and it is the one that outlives this topic)*

> **A repeated shape is a symptom of a single-use surface. Count the surfaces before rewriting the words.**

The six identical endings were not six writers being lazy. The ending is **one grey italic paragraph, read once, gone** — `$("epi").textContent`, `.end .epi` at `index.html:381`, no `white-space` rule anywhere so even a line break collapses. Given a slot that small, that dim and that unrepeatable, with six businesses to close and two languages to close them in, **a form is the rational thing to write.** That is why the word "Saturday" appeared six times, and why removing the word leaves the template standing, repainted.

The same trap is all over this project once it has a name:

- **90 codex paragraphs**, one per decision node, rendered at exactly one place (`engine/engine.js:4189`) inside the quest card. `pendingAt` drops an answered quest from its person, and there is no codex browser anywhere. Written once, shown once, destroyed — and the only repetition in the entire game is that **a wrong answer buys you a re-read.**
- **`DOCS.accept`** — *"Acceptance Criteria & Error Budget"*, with fill-in blanks (*"It is working when ___ (their number, their units)"*). By a clear margin the best teaching artifact in the tree. It is in no `READS` entry, no person hands it, it has no poster and no toast. It is reachable only by climbing the stairs in HQ into a world with zero quests and scrolling inside another document.
- **Three real learning surfaces that are not a quest** — the old lead's desk file with its ten-word glossary, the six per-district deliverables built live from your own decisions, and the decision report. All three are behind Settings or behind that staircase, and **none appears on the city plan**, because `MAPMARK=["work","host"]` deliberately omits the engine's third kind.

The rule of thumb that follows: **anything a player can only meet once is scenery.** If a surface is worth writing 90 paragraphs into, it is worth being able to open twice.

### 7.2 · El conteo manda — the count is the canon

> **When the game closes a chapter on a count, the fiction may only name what the count guarantees.**

`chClosed` ends a district at `need`, so three of eight quests are always unplayed when the ending speaks. Every sentence that names what happened in those three is fiction about an afternoon the player did not have — and it is the **default** path, not an edge case. This generalises past endings: the arithmetic is the canon, and prose that outruns it is a bug with no test behind it. Either the count guarantees the scene (`finale:`), or the scene does not name it (R2).

### 7.3 · Sameness is not the absence of a rule

> **If no test can turn red when two things are identical, identical is the default.**

`test/bump.js` exists in this repo because *"lockstep is not the same as movement."* Six identical endings are the same hole one level up. Written in full as the meta-rule in §2, because it is the literal answer to the owner's second question.

## 8 · RECOMMENDATIONS — wanted by the room, deliberately not in the work

Each names its cheapest real route so nobody re-derives it.

1. **La carta, the handed form only.** The letter belongs in a hand, not on a desk.
