# For AJ — what your game sounds like 🎧

Hi AJ. Nobody is picking this for you, and **you can change your mind later without anyone rebuilding
anything** — it's a setting, like changing a ringtone, not a thing that gets baked in. So answer with
whatever you feel like today, and change it in a month when the vibe is different.

**You don't have to read all of this.** Skip to the bold questions and pick a letter. Everything else
is just there in case you want to know what you're choosing between.

---

## Question 1 · Is there sound at all, and what kind?

This is the real question, and it's not "which song." There are genuinely different things a cooking
game can sound like, and they feel completely different to play.

**A · Music you put on.** A track plays while you cook. Like having something on in the background
while you're actually in a kitchen. You'd pick the track (see question 2).

**B · Just the kitchen.** No music. The knife on the board, oil when something hits the pan, the lid
going on, water starting to move. Nothing else. It sounds like the room you're in.
*This is what a game called Venba does, and it is the reason people say it feels like cooking rather
than like a puzzle.*

**C · Both, and you choose per session.** A switch on the screen: music, or kitchen sounds, or
silence. You decide each time you sit down depending on what you want.

**D · Silence.** Genuinely a real answer and not a cop-out — plenty of people play with the sound off
and put their own thing on. If this is you, say so and we won't build any of it.

> **Pick A, B, C or D.** If you're not sure: C is the one that keeps every other answer open, and
> it's the one we'd build if you don't have a strong feeling.

---

## Question 2 · If there's music — whose, and how much?

Only answer this if you picked A or C.

**A · A track you send us.** You name a song or send a file, and that's what plays. Simplest, and it's
literally your taste in your game.
*Worth knowing: if the game is ever shared outside the family, a commercial song is a problem — not a
rule, just a thing that would need solving then. Nothing stops it now.*

**B · A short loop we write for it.** Something made for this game, a minute or two, that loops
without getting annoying. You'd say the feeling — "warm evening", "rainy afternoon", "kind of sad but
nice" — and hear a couple of versions and pick.

**C · A few tracks that change with what you're doing.** Quieter while you're chopping, warmer when
something's simmering, different when you're reading the book. More work, but the game would feel
like it's paying attention to you.

**D · A playlist you swap whenever.** The game has a slot and you drop whatever you want in it, and
change it as often as you like.

> **Pick one.** And if you picked A or D, **tell us one song you'd put in right now** — not as a
> commitment, just so there's something real in there instead of a placeholder.

## Question 3 · The small one

**Should the game make a sound when something goes RIGHT?** A little note when a dish comes together,
the way a game normally rewards you.

**A · Yes, a small one.** · **B · No — the kitchen sounds are enough.** · **C · Yes, but let me turn
it off.**

*Why we're asking separately: it's the one sound that's about the game rather than about the kitchen,
and it's the first thing that would make it feel like a game instead of like cooking. Some people want
that and some people really don't.*
