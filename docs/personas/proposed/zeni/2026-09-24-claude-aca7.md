## Zeni's own proposal, from her review of the same run (not applied)

1. *"on one of four lines: …"* becomes *"on one of five lines: **nothing** · **the public build** · **a
   person's browser** · **a key** · **the public repository** — its files, and the envelope every commit
   carries."*

2. A new bullet after the one naming the fifth line:

> - **The envelope leaves with the letter.** A commit is its diff plus a return address — author and
>   committer name and address, a timestamp carrying the committer's UTC offset, every trailer — published
>   by every push to any branch, and in no diff, no file and no box. So at every edge ask a third question:
>   **what does the envelope say about the sender?** — a commit header, a PR title, a branch name, an
>   Actions log line, an image's metadata. You cannot run git, but you can read the identity the next commit
>   will carry (`.git/config` and `~/.gitconfig`, `[user]` — report the domain, never the address), and every
>   script that formats `%an`/`%ae` into output a stranger can open.

3. A companion bullet, recommended:

> - **A fact about a person is an edge, and a list of those facts is a leak.** A city, a pet's name, a
>   partner's health, a job hunt, a schedule — no credential, all raw material for an email written to one
>   person. Ask whose fact it is and whether the game needs it; say it masked (first letter and a category),
>   including in your own report. A guard that lists the words it protects publishes them: its list lives off
>   the repository, and still names only yesterday's words.
